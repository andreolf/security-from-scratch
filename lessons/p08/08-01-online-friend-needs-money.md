:::story Daniel on the oil platform
Erika is 71 and widowed. She lives in Solothurn. On Facebook, a man called Daniel starts writing to her. He is kind and funny, he writes every morning, and after three weeks he says he loves her. He is an engineer on an oil platform, so he can never video call.

Then: “There was an accident. I need 3,000 francs for the hospital, my card is blocked.” Erika feels the pull to help. Instead, she tells her friend Vreni. Vreni searches Daniel’s photo online. It belongs to a dentist in Ohio, under another name. Erika blocks Daniel. It hurts, and she is glad she asked.

*Erika, Daniel and Vreni are made up.*
:::end

:::remember
**Never send money, gifts or gift cards to someone you have never met in person, however real the feelings are.**
:::end

## How it works

The Swiss Federal Office for Cyber Security (BACS) explains that romance scammers create fake profiles on social media and dating sites to fake a love relationship. They often say they can’t meet because they are abroad, for example. Once there is trust, they ask for money ([BACS: Romance Scam](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/romance-scam-de)).

The Zurich cantonal police add that the scammers often pose as army generals, doctors or engineers working abroad. They send charming messages every day and make promises up to marriage, before asking for money for an emergency ([cybercrimepolice.ch: Liebesbetrug](https://cybercrimepolice.ch/de/faelle/liebesbetrug)).

## It costs a great deal

BACS writes that, alongside online investment fraud, romance scams cause some of the highest losses it sees ([BACS: Woche 14](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/26w14-de)). In the US, people aged 60 and over reported 10,188 romance and confidence scams to the FBI in 2025, with losses of about $584 million ([FBI IC3: 2025 report](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf)).

Scammers now use AI too. BACS describes a case where the “partner” sent AI-made pictures of himself in a hospital bed, with a cup bearing the victim’s name on the bedside table, to make the story of an expensive treatment believable ([BACS: Woche 14](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/26w14-de)).

## Warning signs

- **Love comes very fast**, before you have ever met ([cybercrimepolice.ch](https://cybercrimepolice.ch/de/faelle/liebesbetrug)).
- **There is always a reason you can’t meet**: they live abroad, work on an oil rig or are in the military ([FTC: Romance scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-romance-scams)).
- **They ask for money** for a ticket, a visa, a hospital bill or “fees”, or they offer to help you start investing in crypto ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-romance-scams)).
- **They tell you how to pay**: by transfer, gift cards, a payment app or cryptocurrency ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-romance-scams)).

## Check the photo

The police suggest checking the person’s photos with an image search, such as Google’s. If the same photos appear elsewhere on the internet, under other names, that is a strong sign of fraud ([cybercrimepolice.ch](https://cybercrimepolice.ch/de/faelle/liebesbetrug)). The FTC also suggests searching the job they claim plus the word “scammer”, for example “oil rig scammer” ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-romance-scams)).
