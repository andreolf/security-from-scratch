:::story Sarah the coach
Monika is 68 and lives in Zug. After clicking an advert, she ends up in a WhatsApp group run by “Sarah, investment coach”. Sarah is patient and kind. She helps Monika install a trading app and invest CHF 500. Within weeks, the app shows CHF 2,100.

“Add 10,000 now,” Sarah writes, “and you’ll double it before Christmas.” Monika asks to withdraw CHF 500 first, to be sure. Suddenly there is a “tax” to pay. Monika stops, calls her bank and goes to the police. The CHF 500 is lost, but not the 10,000.

*Monika and Sarah are made up.*
:::end

:::remember
**A balance in an app is not money in your bank. If you can’t withdraw without paying first, stop and pay nothing more.**
:::end

## How it works

The FBI describes the pattern. Scammers make contact through text messages, social media, adverts or dating apps, then quickly move the conversation to a messaging app. Victims are introduced to “investment groups” that claim to be insiders. They are persuaded to send money to fake platforms or apps, shown fake profits and even offered loans to invest more. When they try to withdraw, they are charged “taxes” and “fees”, before the scammers disappear ([FBI IC3: 2025 report](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf)).

The FBI says cryptocurrency investment fraud caused the highest losses reported by Americans in 2025: $7.2 billion ([FBI IC3](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf)).

## The friendly adviser

The FTC explains that the company taking your money isn’t really investing it, and isn’t real. You see fake reports showing your money growing, and you’re urged to invest more ([FTC: Investment scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/investment-scams)). The FTC adds: if someone you meet on a dating site or app wants to show you how to invest in crypto, that’s a scam ([FTC: Cryptocurrency and scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-cryptocurrency-scams)).

In Switzerland, the Zurich cantonal police describe adverts with celebrities leading to trading platforms, a call from a “broker” within minutes, fake early profits and blocked withdrawals. They advise checking whether the firm has a FINMA licence and never giving anyone remote access to your computer ([cybercrimepolice.ch: Promibilder](https://cybercrimepolice.ch/de/faelle/online-anlagebetrueger-missbrauchen-prominente)).

## Three rules

1. **Don’t invest with someone who contacted you.** You choose where to invest, not a stranger in a chat.
2. **Never install an app or give remote access for a “coach”** (lesson 05.04).
3. **Test with your own bank, not their screen.** If money can’t come back to your own account without a fee, it was never there.

:::practice scenes scene=trading

:::helper
Warning signs for helpers: a relative suddenly talks about an online coach, a new trading app, or large profits. Ask to see the app’s company name and look it up together on FINMA’s warning list. Stay kind: they may already have put money in, and shame makes people hide it.
:::end
