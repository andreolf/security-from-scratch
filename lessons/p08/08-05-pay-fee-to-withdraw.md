:::story The release fee
Peter is 74 and lives in Lucerne. For a year, his trading app showed his savings growing to over CHF 60,000. When he asks to withdraw, a message says: “Pay 10% tax first, then the money is released.” He pays CHF 6,000. Then: “An anti-money-laundering check costs CHF 4,000.”

Peter’s neighbour, a retired bank clerk, says gently: “Peter, real banks take fees out of the money. They don’t ask you to send more.” Peter stops paying and reports it. Months later a “lawyer” calls, offering to recover everything for a fee. This time Peter knows. He hangs up.

*Peter is made up.*
:::end

:::remember
**If you have to pay before you can get your money out, the money isn’t there. Stop, pay nothing more, and report it.**
:::end

## The fee is the last trick

The FBI describes how, when victims of fake investment platforms try to withdraw, “they will be charged taxes and fees as a final attempt” to take more money before the scammers disappear ([FBI IC3: 2025 report](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf)). The FTC explains that on a fake investment site, you won’t be able to withdraw your money at all, or only if you pay high fees ([FTC: Cryptocurrency and scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-cryptocurrency-scams)).

The Swiss Federal Office for Cyber Security describes the same pattern with fake celebrity investment adverts: when you try to withdraw, you are asked for more “fees” ([BACS: Woche 7](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/25w07-de)).

## The second scam: recovery offers

People who have already been scammed are often targeted again. The FTC calls refund and recovery scams “the worst of the worst”. Scammers buy lists of people who lost money, then pose as a government agency, a consumer group or a law firm, and ask for an upfront “processing fee” or “tax” ([FTC: Refund and recovery scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/refund-and-recovery-scams)).

FINMA, the Swiss financial regulator, warns that lists of defrauded investors are sold to new fraudsters, and that victims usually lose money a second time. FINMA generally does not contact private individuals, and it is never involved in offers to recover losses ([FINMA: Beispiele für Anlagebetrug](https://www.finma.ch/de/finma-public/wie-sie-sich-schuetzen-koennen/beispiele-fuer-anlagebetrug/)). Swiss Crime Prevention adds: if supposed helpers such as “Interpol”, a foreign police force or “victims’ lawyers” contact you, don’t react ([SKP: Romance Scam](https://www.skppsc.ch/de/themen/internet/romance-scam/)).

The FTC is clear: government agencies and real organisations will never ask for money to help you get a refund ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/refund-and-recovery-scams)).

## What to do

- **Pay nothing more**, whatever the pressure ([cybercrimepolice.ch](https://cybercrimepolice.ch/de/faelle/liebesbetrug)).
- **Keep everything**: messages, emails, the app’s name, payment receipts ([cybercrimepolice.ch](https://cybercrimepolice.ch/de/faelle/liebesbetrug)).
- **Call your bank and report it to the police.** Phase 10 explains the steps in the right order.

:::helper
If a relative says they “just need to pay one fee” to get their money back, don’t argue about the amount. Ask: “Would a real bank ask you to send money to get your own money?” Then offer to go to the police with them.
:::end
