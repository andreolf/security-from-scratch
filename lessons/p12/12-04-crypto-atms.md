:::story The station machine
Heidi is 76 and lives in Olten. A man calling from “the bank’s security department” says her account was hacked. He tells her to take out CHF 9,000 and stay on the phone, then directs her to a machine at the station and sends her a QR code.

At the machine, a young man waiting behind her sees she is upset and on the phone. He says, kindly: “Is someone telling you to put cash in there? That’s a scam.” Heidi hangs up and calls her bank on the number on her card.

*Heidi is made up.*
:::end

:::remember
**No real bank, police or government will ever tell you to put money into crypto, or into a crypto ATM, to protect it.**
:::end

## How the scam works

The US Federal Trade Commission says only scammers demand payment in cryptocurrency, and only scammers say you must buy crypto to protect your money. Government impersonators, it says, keep you on the phone, send you to a crypto ATM, and give you a QR code that sends the money into their wallet ([FTC: Cryptocurrency and scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-cryptocurrency-scams)).

The FTC’s 2026 report on older consumers describes people told that a security issue put their accounts at risk, then directed to transfer money, mail cash, or deposit cash into Bitcoin ATMs to keep it “safe” ([FTC: Protecting Older Consumers, p. 25](https://search.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/2026-older-adults-report-10192026.pdf)).

## How big it is

In its 2025 report, the FBI’s Internet Crime Complaint Center counted 13,460 complaints involving crypto ATMs, with $389 million in losses, up 58% from 2024. People aged 60 and over made 6,188 of those complaints and lost $257,466,130 ([FBI IC3 2025 report, p. 54](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf)).

## Why the money can’t come back

Crypto payments usually can’t be reversed, and they don’t have the legal protections of card payments, the FTC explains ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-cryptocurrency-scams)). Once the cash is in the machine and sent, it is very hard to get back.

## What to do

1. **Hang up** on anyone who tells you to take out cash, buy crypto, or use a crypto ATM.
2. **Call your bank** on the number on your card (lesson 10.02).
3. **Tell staff nearby** if you are at a machine and unsure.
4. **Report it** to the police (lesson 10.03).

:::helper
If you see someone at a crypto ATM who seems stressed and is on the phone, a calm, kind question can stop a scam: “Is someone on the phone telling you to do this?”
:::end
