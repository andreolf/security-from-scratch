:::story The helpful stranger
Erich is 71 and lives in Schaffhausen. He posts a question in a public crypto chat group: “My wallet shows the wrong balance.” Within a minute, “Official Wallet Support” sends him a private message, very polite, with a link to “sync” his wallet.

The page asks for his 12 words. Erich remembers this lesson, closes the page, and blocks the account. His balance was just slow to update.

*Erich is made up.*
:::end

:::remember
**Nobody real will ever ask for your secret phrase. Not support, not a website, not a pop-up, not a caller.**
:::end

## The one rule

ethereum.org is clear: never, for any reason, share your recovery phrase or private keys. No legitimate service, support agent or website will ever ask you for them ([ethereum.org: Security](https://ethereum.org/en/security/)).

:::figure nobody-asks
Who may ask for your phrase? Nobody.
:::end

## How they ask

- **Fake support.** ethereum.org describes support scams: scammers pretend to be support staff for wallets or exchanges. They often find people asking questions in public chat groups and then message them privately, building trust before asking for keys or funds ([ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/security/)).
- **Free tokens.** An unknown token appears in your wallet, with a link to “claim” it. ethereum.org warns that these airdrop scams lead to a site that asks you to approve a transaction ([ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/security/)).
- **Giveaways.** “Send us crypto and we send double back.” ethereum.org says giveaways are always scams ([ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/security/)).
- **Celebrity videos.** Fake livestreams with famous faces promising crypto (lesson 06.06).

## What to do

1. Never type or say your phrase, to anyone, anywhere.
2. Ignore private messages offering help. Find help yourself on the wallet maker’s own website, typed in by you.
3. Block and report the account.
4. If you already shared it, go to lesson 12.06 now.
