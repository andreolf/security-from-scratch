:::story An afternoon with Grandpa
Noah, 16, spends a rainy Sunday with his grandfather Ernst, 79. Together they turn on the password manager on Ernst’s phone. They start with just one account: his email.

Ernst types his new passphrase himself, and the phone offers to save it. Noah looks away while he types. Afterwards, the phone shows a list of tips: two old passwords are “used on multiple websites”. “We’ll fix those next Sunday,” says Ernst. One step at a time.
:::end

:::remember
**Start with one account, your email. Then add the others one by one, at your own pace.**
:::end

## Before you start

- Make sure your phone has a **screen lock** (a code, your face or your fingerprint) that only you know. It protects the password manager.
- Have **one account** ready to start with. Email is the best choice, because lesson 03.05 explains why it is your master key.
- Set aside about **twenty minutes**. There is no hurry.

:::figure pm-setup
The set-up in four steps: lock, turn on, save one, check.
:::end

:::try Turn on your phone’s password manager
--- iPhone
1. Open **Settings**, tap **General**, then tap **AutoFill & Passwords**.
2. Turn on **AutoFill Passwords and Passkeys**. Your iPhone can now fill in saved passwords for you.
3. Open the **Passwords** app (on iOS 18 or later) to see the passwords you have saved.
4. In the Passwords app, tap **Security** to see recommendations to change weak passwords.

Checked against: [Apple Support: Use the Passwords app on your iPhone](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120758), [Apple Support: Use passwords on iPhone](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/use-passwords-iphd18e98624/ios) and [Apple Support: Find saved passwords and passkeys](https://support.apple.com/en-us/104955).

--- Android
1. Open **Chrome**. Tap **More** (three dots), then **Settings**.
2. Tap **Autofill and passwords**, then **Google Password Manager**.
3. At the bottom right, tap **Settings**, and make sure **Offer to save passwords** is on.
4. To add a password yourself: go back, tap **Add password** below the search bar, enter the website, your username and the password, then tap **Save**.
5. To check your saved passwords for problems, use **Password Checkup**.

Menus vary a little between phone brands. Checked against: [Google Chrome Help: Manage passwords (Android)](https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/95606?co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid&hl=en), [Google Account Help: Google Password Manager](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/6208650?co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid&hl=en) and [Google Account Help: Password Checkup](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/9457609).
:::end

## Saving your first password

The easiest way: sign in to a website or app as usual. When your phone asks “Save password?”, tap **Save**. Next time, the phone fills it in for you. Chrome, for example, offers to save your password by default ([Google Chrome Help](https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/95606?co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid&hl=en)).

When you create a new account or change a password, let the password manager suggest a strong one. You don’t need to remember it. CISA explains that password managers can generate strong passwords for you ([CISA](https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world/use-strong-passwords)).

## Next steps, at your own pace

- Each week, add or improve one more account: bank, shopping, health insurance.
- When the password manager warns about a reused or leaked password, change that one.
- Never read out a password or a code to anyone who calls you. A password manager never needs you to.

:::exercise The helper checklist
With your helper, tick each line when it is done:

1. Phone has a screen lock only I know.
2. Password manager is turned on.
3. My email password is saved in it.
4. I know how to open the list of saved passwords.
5. I have looked at the safety tips (Security or Password Checkup).
:::solution
If all five are ticked, you have done the most important part. Many people stop at step 3 the first time, and that is fine. Come back to steps 4 and 5 another day.
:::end

:::helper
Let your relative do the tapping and typing. Look away when they type a password, and say so: it shows that passwords are private, even from family. If they use a separate password manager app, check it is the official one in the app store. Write down which password manager they use, so you can help later.
:::end
