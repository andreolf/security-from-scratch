:::story The birthday phone
For his 78th birthday, Bruno gets a new phone from his children. His daughter Lea doesn’t just hand it over. They spend Sunday morning setting it up together: a passcode he can remember, updates on, bigger text, two-step sign-in, and Lea as his recovery contact.

Bruno writes the steps in his notebook. “Now I know what’s on it,” he says.

*Bruno and Lea are made up.*
:::end

:::remember
**Five things on every new phone: screen lock, automatic updates, two-step sign-in, a recovery contact or codes, and comfortable text size.**
:::end

## Why the first day matters

A new phone is a clean start. Settings chosen together on day one stay that way, and your relative knows what was set and why.

:::figure new-phone
Five things to set up on a new phone.
:::end

## Step by step

:::try Set up the phone together
--- iPhone
1. **Screen lock:** open **Settings**, tap **Face ID & Passcode** (or **Touch ID & Passcode**), then **Turn Passcode On**. Apple says a custom code is the most secure option ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/set-a-passcode-iph14a867ae/ios)).
2. **Updates:** **Settings** > **General** > **Software Update** > **Automatic Updates**, and turn on automatic install ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118575)). More in lesson 05.02.
3. **Two-step sign-in:** **Settings** > **your name** > **Sign-In & Security** > **Two-Factor Authentication** ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102660)). More in lesson 04.03.
4. **Recovery contact:** **Settings** > **your name** > **Sign-In & Security** > **Recovery Contacts** ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102641)).
5. **Text size:** **Settings** > **Accessibility** > **Display & Text Size** > **Larger Text** ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/make-text-easier-to-read-iph3c076905a/ios)).
--- Android
1. **Screen lock:** open the **Settings** app, tap **Security**, then **Screen lock**, and choose a PIN, pattern or password. Google recommends a PIN of at least 6 digits ([Android Help](https://support.google.com/android/answer/9079129?hl=en)).
2. **Updates:** most updates install automatically; to check, open **Settings** > **System** > **Software updates** ([Android Help](https://support.google.com/android/answer/7680439?hl=en)). More in lesson 05.02.
3. **Two-step sign-in:** **Settings** > **Google** > **Manage your Google Account** > **Security & sign-in** > **Turn on 2-Step Verification** ([Google Account Help](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/185839?co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid&hl=en)). More in lesson 04.03.
4. **Backup codes:** in the same place, under **2-Step Verification**, get backup codes and keep them on paper ([Google Account Help](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/1187538?co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid&hl=en)).
5. **Text size:** in **Settings**, search for **Font size** and move the slider ([Android Accessibility Help](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/11183305)).
:::end

Menu names differ a little between models and brands. If you can’t find a setting, use the search box at the top of Settings.

## Also worth doing

- Add trusted numbers to Contacts, with clear names like “Bank – block card” (lesson 00.04).
- Install apps only from the official App Store or Google Play (lesson 05.05).
- Turn on spam call filtering (lesson 01.04 shows where).

:::exercise Make a setup card
On a sheet of paper, write the five things: screen lock, updates, two-step sign-in, recovery contact or codes, text size. Next to each, write where it is in Settings on this phone, in your own words.
:::solution
Keep the card with the phone’s box and papers. Next time a phone is replaced, or after a big update moves things around, the card shows what to check.
:::end

:::helper
Write the new passcode on paper and keep it at home in a safe place, not in the phone box. Don’t keep a copy yourself unless your relative asks you to.
:::end
