:::story The phone that stayed his
Luca is 45 and lives in Bern. Every Christmas he used to “fix” his mother’s phone in ten minutes. By February she couldn’t remember what he had changed, and called him for every pop-up.

This year he tries another way. They sit at the kitchen table. She holds the phone, he points. They write each step on a card. It takes longer, but in March she turns on two-step sign-in for her email herself.

*Luca and his mother are made up.*
:::end

:::remember
**Do it together, not for them. They hold the phone, they keep their passwords, and they decide.**
:::end

## Why taking over backfires

Doing everything quickly for someone feels kind. But they then can’t repeat it, don’t know what changed, and may feel less able to judge a scam themselves. Scammers look for people who feel unsure and alone (lesson 08.02). The Swiss Federal Office for Cyber Security (BACS) explains that romance scammers deliberately pick people who are lonely ([BACS: Woche 32](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/25w32-de)). Being a patient, regular helper is part of the protection.

## Good helping habits

- **Ask first:** “Shall we look at your phone safety together?”
- **Let them tap.** You point and explain. Go slowly.
- **Explain why** in one sentence: “Updates fix holes that criminals use.”
- **Write it down.** A short paper note of each step helps later.
- **Their passwords stay theirs.** Help them set up a password manager (lesson 03.03) instead of keeping a list yourself.
- **No blame.** If they clicked something, thank them for telling you.

## A trusted contact at the bank

Some banks and investment firms let you name a trusted contact person. In the US, the regulator FINRA explains that a trusted contact can be contacted by the firm, for example about possible financial exploitation, but cannot make trades or decisions on the account, and is not a power of attorney ([FINRA](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/older-investors-financial-exploitation)). Ask your own bank what it offers. Your relative decides whether to use it.

:::practice scenes scene=helper

:::helper
If you are the helper: set a regular, relaxed time, such as one afternoon a month. Short and often works better than one long session.
:::end

:::quiz
- q: You are helping your mother with her phone. What is best?
  options: ["Do it all quickly yourself", "Do it together, and let her tap", "Tell her to figure it out"]
  answer: 1
  explain: Together means she learns and stays in charge.
- q: Who should know her passwords?
  options: ["Her, stored safely in a password manager", "You, on a list in your wallet", "Everyone in the family group chat"]
  answer: 0
  explain: Her accounts, her passwords, stored safely.
- q: She clicked a bad link and tells you. What do you say first?
  options: ["“How could you?”", "“Thank you for telling me. Let’s fix it.”", "Nothing"]
  answer: 1
  explain: Thanks, not blame, keeps her telling you.
:::end
