:::story The doctor and the parcel
Martin is 71. He was a doctor for forty years. He reads two newspapers every day.

One morning his phone shows a text: “Your parcel is waiting. Pay the small fee today, or it goes back.” Martin is expecting a book from his daughter. He taps the link without thinking.

The page asks for his card number. Something feels rushed. He stops, closes the page and calls his daughter. She sent nothing by post. The text was fake.
:::end

Martin is clever and careful. He still nearly fell for it. That is not a weakness. It is exactly how scams are designed.

## It is not about intelligence

Scammers don’t try to out-think you. They try to stop you from thinking at all. They do that with three feelings.

- **Fear.** “Your grandson is in trouble.” “Your account has been hacked.” “The police need you.”
- **Hurry.** “Within the hour.” “Today only.” “Don’t hang up.”
- **Secrecy.** “Don’t tell anyone.” “Your bank staff can’t be trusted.”

When we are afraid and rushed, the calm, thinking part of us goes quiet. Any person, of any age, reacts this way. Scammers know it, and they build every trick around it.

:::end

## This happens to many people

You are not alone, and you are not the only one who is targeted. In the second half of 2025, the Swiss Federal Office for Cyber Security received 29,006 reports from the public, and just over half were about fraud ([BACS half-year report](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/hjb-25-2-de)). In the United States, people aged 60 and over reported losses of more than $3 billion to the Federal Trade Commission in 2025 ([FTC report on older consumers](https://search.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/2026-older-adults-report-10192026.pdf)).

Most people who are scammed never tell anyone, often because they feel ashamed. Talking about it openly is how we stop it. If it has happened to you or someone you know, it is not your fault.

:::remember
**Fear, hurry or secrecy is your signal to stop. The feeling itself is the warning sign.**
:::end

## Your new habit

You don’t need to know every trick. You only need to notice the feeling. When a call, a message or a visitor makes you afraid, rushed or secretive, say to yourself: “This is the moment to stop.”

Then use the three golden rules from the next lesson.

:::practice pressure-spotter

:::helper
Never tease anyone who almost fell for a scam, and never say “How could you?” Shame keeps people silent, and silence helps scammers. Say instead: “Thank you for telling me. Those people are professionals. Let’s check it together.”
:::end
