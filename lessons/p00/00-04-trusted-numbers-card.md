:::story The card on the fridge
Pierre is 76 and lives in Lausanne. His daughter made him a small card with five numbers on it: his bank, his daughter, his son, his neighbour and the police. It hangs on the fridge, next to the phone.

One day a caller says he is from Pierre’s bank. Pierre doesn’t argue. He says: “I will call you back.” He hangs up, looks at the card and calls the bank’s real number. The bank knows nothing about the call.

Pierre didn’t need to know whether the caller was real. The card did the work.
:::end

## Why a card helps

Rule three says: check on a number you already have. That is easy when the number is right in front of you. In a stressful moment, nobody wants to search through drawers or the internet. Searching online can even lead you to fake numbers: Swiss authorities report scammers using fake search-engine adverts ([BACS half-year report](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/hjb-25-2-de)).

A trusted-numbers card puts the real numbers in one place. You make it once, in a calm moment. Then it is ready when you need it.

## Where to find the real numbers

- **Your bank:** the number printed on the back of your bank card, or on a bank statement or letter.
- **Family and friends:** numbers you got from them in person.
- **Your helper:** the person you agree to call first when something feels wrong.
- **Emergency numbers:** in Switzerland, call 117 for the police ([cybercrimepolice.ch](https://cybercrimepolice.ch/de/faelle/schockanruf-durch-falsche-polizisten)). The number 112 works free of charge across the European Union and also in Switzerland ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/112)). In the United States, call 911 in an emergency ([US Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/elderjustice/find-help-or-report-abuse)).

Never copy a number from a call, a text message, an email or a search advert. Those are exactly the places scammers use.

:::remember
**Write the real numbers down in a calm moment, so you never have to search for them in a stressful one.**
:::end

:::practice trusted-card

:::try Save a trusted number in your contacts
A number saved in your contacts can be called with one tap. Save your bank’s number from the back of your card.

--- iPhone
1. Open the **Contacts** app.
2. Tap **+** (the plus sign).
3. Type a name, such as “My bank (from my card)”, and the phone number.
4. Tap **Done**.
5. To call it with fewer taps, open the contact, scroll down and tap **Add to Favorites**.

Checked against: [Apple Support: Add and use contact information on iPhone](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/add-and-use-contact-information-iph3e0ca2db/ios) and [Apple Support: Use contacts from the Phone app](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/use-contacts-from-the-phone-app-iph9b90d0aba/ios).

--- Android
1. Open the **Contacts** app.
2. Tap **Add** (usually at the bottom right).
3. Type a name, such as “My bank (from my card)”, and the phone number.
4. Tap **Save**.

Checked against: [Google Contacts Help: Add, find & edit contacts on Android](https://support.google.com/contacts/answer/1069522?co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid&hl=en).
:::end

:::helper
Make the card together, and copy the bank number from the actual card, not from memory or a website. Laminate it or put it in a plastic sleeve. Save the same numbers in the phone’s contacts with clear names, and check once a year that they are still right.
:::end
