:::story The man at the door
Hanna is 80. On Thursday evening the doorbell rings. A friendly man in a jacket says he is from the water company. “There is a problem with the pipes in your street. I need to check your kitchen right away.”

Hanna feels rushed. Then she remembers her three rules. She says: “Please wait outside. I will call the water company first.” She closes the door and calls the number on her last bill.

There was no problem in her street. When she looks out again, the man has gone.
:::end

## Three rules for everything

You don’t need a different rule for every trick. These three work for a call, a text message, an email, a pop-up on your screen, or a stranger at the door.

:::figure three-rules
Stop, hang up, check: the same three steps every time.
:::end

**1. Stop.** Notice the feeling: fear, hurry or secrecy. That feeling is your cue. You don’t have to decide anything now. Nothing real is so urgent that you can’t take five minutes.

**2. Hang up.** End the call, close the message, or shut the door. You are allowed to. You don’t need to explain or be polite. A real bank, a real police officer or a real company will understand.

**3. Check.** Contact the person or company yourself, on a number or address you already have. Use the number on the back of your bank card, on a letter or bill, or in your own contacts. Never use a number, link or address that the caller or message gave you.

## Why “hang up” matters so much

While you are on the phone, the caller is in control. They can keep talking, add pressure and stop you from thinking. Hanging up gives you back control. The US Federal Communications Commission puts it in four words: “Don’t Hang On, Hang Up!” ([FCC: Caller ID spoofing](https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/spoofing)).

Swiss police give the same advice. If a caller says they are the police and asks about money, hang up and call 117 yourself. On a landline, wait for the dial tone before you dial, so you are not still connected to the caller ([cybercrimepolice.ch](https://cybercrimepolice.ch/de/faelle/falsche-polizisten-rufen-wieder-aeltere-personen-an)).

:::remember
**Stop. Hang up. Check, on a number you already have.**
:::end

## What if it was real?

Sometimes the call or message is real. That’s fine. If you call your bank on the number on your card and it was real, they will help you straight away. Checking costs you a few minutes. Not checking can cost you your savings.

:::exercise Say it out loud
Read the three rules out loud three times: “Stop. Hang up. Check.” Then write them on a small note and stick it next to your phone or on the fridge.
:::solution
Saying the rules aloud helps you remember them when you feel rushed. Many people also like to write one calm sentence under the rules, such as: “I never decide about money on the phone.”
:::end

:::helper
Practise the rules together in a relaxed moment, not right after a scare. Agree that the person can always call you to check, at any hour, and that you will never be annoyed. Knowing they can call you makes it much easier to hang up on a stranger.
:::end
