Welcome. This course is for anyone who wants to feel safer with a phone, a tablet or a computer. You don’t need any experience. You only need a few minutes at a time.

Scam calls, fake messages and forgotten passwords can feel worrying. The good news: a few simple habits protect you from most tricks. This course teaches those habits, one small step at a time.

## How each lesson works

Every lesson has the same shape, so you always know what comes next.

- **A short story.** The stories are made up, and they are labelled. Each one follows a pattern that police and consumer agencies have written about.
- **The one thing to remember.** A single sentence in a highlighted box. If you remember nothing else, remember that.
- **A simple picture.** Every picture also has a “Read the picture as text” button, so nothing is hidden in an image.
- **Try it on your own phone.** Step-by-step instructions for iPhone and Android, checked against Apple’s and Google’s own help pages.
- **Three questions.** They are not graded. Pick an answer and read why.
- **For family helpers.** A box with tips for the person who helps you with your phone.

## Make it comfortable for you

At the top of every page there are two buttons:

- **Bigger text** makes all the words on this site larger.
- **Dark** or **Light** switches the colours. Light is the default. Choose what is easiest for your eyes.

At the start of every lesson you will find a large button: **Read this lesson to me**. Your phone or computer then reads the lesson aloud, paragraph by paragraph. The paragraph being read is outlined. Press the button again to stop. The voice comes from your own device. Nothing is sent anywhere.

There is no clock anywhere in this course. You can stop at any time and come back later. Your progress is saved only in this browser. You don’t need an account.

:::remember
**Take your time. Nothing on this site is a race, and you can always come back.**
:::end

:::try Make the text bigger on your phone
Bigger text on your phone helps with every app, not just this course.

--- iPhone
1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap **Accessibility**, then **Display & Text Size**.
3. Tap **Larger Text**.
4. Drag the slider to the right until the text is comfortable. For even bigger text, turn on **Larger Accessibility Sizes**.
5. You can also turn on **Bold Text** on the Display & Text Size screen.

Checked against: [Apple Support: Make text easier to read on iPhone](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/make-text-easier-to-read-iph3c076905a/ios).

--- Android
1. Open your phone’s **Settings** app.
2. Search for **Font size**.
3. Drag the slider until the text is comfortable.

Android phones differ a little between brands. If you can’t find it, search in Settings for “Font size”.

Checked against: [Google Android Accessibility Help: Change text & display settings](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/11183305).
:::end

:::helper
Sit next to the person, not in front of the phone. Let them do the tapping. Show the **Bigger text** and **Read this lesson to me** buttons first. If they like, set up a home-screen shortcut to this site together, so it opens with one tap.
:::end

:::exercise Your own starting point
Take a piece of paper. Write down one thing about phones or the internet that worries you. Keep the paper. At the end of the course, look at it again.
:::solution
There is no wrong answer. Many people write things like “I don’t know if a call from my bank is real” or “I use the same password everywhere”. Both have their own lessons in this course: lesson 01.03 and lesson 03.01.
:::end
