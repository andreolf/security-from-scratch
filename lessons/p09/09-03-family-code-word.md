:::story The Sunday lunch
At Sunday lunch in Fribourg, Grandma Lise, 77, brings up a news story about cloned voices. Her son suggests a family code word. The grandchildren laugh, then get into it. After some debate they choose “blue teapot”, after the old teapot in Lise’s kitchen. It isn’t on anyone’s social media.

Three months later Lise gets a call: “Grandma, it’s Noah, I’m in trouble.” She asks: “What’s our word?” Silence, then the line goes dead.

*Lise and Noah are made up.*
:::end

:::remember
**Agree on a family code word face to face. If a call or message asks for money or help in a hurry, ask for the word. No word? Hang up and call back.**
:::end

## Why a code word works

The FBI’s first tip against AI-made fakes is to create a secret word or phrase with your family to check who you are talking to ([FBI IC3: Generative AI PSA](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2024/PSA241203)). The FTC similarly suggests asking a question only the real person would know ([FTC: Fake emergencies](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/scammers-use-fake-emergencies-steal-your-money)).

A voice can be copied (lesson 09.01). A face can be faked (lesson 09.02). But a word that only your family knows, and that was never written down online, can’t be copied from social media.

Setting up and using a family code word.

## Choosing a good word

- **Not guessable.** Not your dog’s name, your street or anything posted online. A scammer may already know a lot about you from social media, the FTC warns ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/scammers-use-fake-emergencies-steal-your-money)).
- **Easy to remember**, even under stress. Two simple words work well, like “blue teapot”.
- **Agreed face to face**, or on a call you started.
- **Everyone who might call for help knows it**: children, grandchildren, the people who care for you.

## Using it

- Ask for the word when a call or message wants **money, secrecy or hurry**.
- **Never give hints.** An excuse instead of the word means: hang up.
- **The word doesn’t replace calling back.** If in doubt, still call the person on the number you know.
- **Never write it in a chat or post it.** If an account is taken over, old messages can be read.

:::try Set up your code word
--- Step by step
1. Pick a moment when the family is together, or call each person yourself.
2. Explain why, in one sentence: “Voices can be copied now, so we need a word only we know.”
3. Choose the word together. Check nobody has posted it anywhere.
4. Agree on the rule: “If anyone calls asking for money, I’ll ask for the word.”
5. Practise once: one person calls, the other asks for the word.
:::end

:::practice scenes scene=codeword

:::helper
You can be the one who suggests it. Keep it warm and light: it is like a family secret, not a sign that anyone is in danger. Repeat the practice call once a year, and agree on a new word if anyone thinks it has leaked.
:::end
