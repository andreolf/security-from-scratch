:::story Marc’s new phone
Silvia is 70 and lives in Neuchâtel. A video call comes from an unknown number. On the screen is her nephew Marc, a bit blurry: “Hi! New phone. Can you lend me 1,500 francs? I’ll explain later.”

Something feels off. His lips don’t quite match his words, and he hardly blinks. Silvia says, “Let me call you back,” ends the call and rings Marc’s usual number. Marc is at home, watching football. He has not changed phones.

*Silvia and Marc are made up.*
:::end

:::remember
**A face on a screen can be faked. If a video call asks for money, end it and call back on the number you know.**
:::end

## What can be faked

The FBI warns that criminals use AI-made video for live video chats with supposed company bosses, police or other authority figures, and to “prove” that an online contact is a real person ([FBI IC3: Generative AI PSA](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2024/PSA241203)).

The Swiss Federal Office for Cyber Security (BACS) explains that videos can be made from photos of real people, for example from social media. Famous people are often misused, for instance in fake investment adverts ([BACS: Woche 49](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/23w49-de)). BACS also reports isolated cases where a cloned boss took part in a video conference to push employees into an urgent payment. For now these are rare and targeted, but BACS expects them to increase ([BACS: Woche 31](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/26w31-de)).

## Signs that may help

The FBI lists possible imperfections in fake images and videos: distorted hands, unrealistic teeth or eyes, irregular faces, odd shadows, lag, a voice that doesn’t match, and unrealistic movements ([FBI IC3](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2024/PSA241203)). BACS adds that lip movements are not yet perfectly in sync, and that fakes often behave unlike the real person, so it’s worth noticing gestures, facial expressions and the way they usually talk ([BACS: Woche 31](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/26w31-de)).

## Don’t rely on spotting

Fakes keep getting better. BACS expects more realistic videos with less effort in future ([BACS: Woche 31](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/26w31-de)). So don’t make your safety depend on noticing a mistake. Use a check that always works:

- **End the call** if it asks for money, passwords or secrecy.
- **Call back** on a number you already have, or look it up yourself.
- **Never give remote access** to someone you met on a call (lesson 05.04).

:::end
