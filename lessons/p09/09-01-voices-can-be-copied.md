:::story Nonna Teresa
Teresa is 82 and lives in Bellinzona. Her phone rings. It is her grandson Luca, crying: “Nonna, I had an accident. I need money for a lawyer. Please don’t tell Mum.” It is his voice, no doubt.

Teresa’s heart races. But her daughter taught her one thing: “If it’s about money, call back.” She says, “I’ll call you right back,” and hangs up. She calls Luca’s usual number. He picks up, at work, perfectly fine.

*Teresa and Luca are made up.*
:::end

:::remember
**A voice can be copied. If a call asks for money or secrecy, hang up and call the person back on the number you already have.**
:::end

## How voice cloning works

Software can now make a [[term:voice-clone|copy of a voice]]. The Swiss Federal Office for Cyber Security (BACS) says that cloning a voice currently needs an audio file of about 10 seconds ([BACS: Woche 31](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/26w31-de)). The FTC explains that with a short audio clip, perhaps from something posted online, and a voice-cloning program, a scammer could sound just like your family member ([FTC: Fake emergencies](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/scammers-use-fake-emergencies-steal-your-money)).

The FBI warns that criminals create short audio clips with a loved one’s voice to pose as a relative in a crisis, asking for money at once or demanding a ransom ([FBI IC3: Generative AI PSA](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2024/PSA241203)). In 2025, people in the US reported losses of over $5 million to such “distress” scams with a likely AI link ([FBI IC3: 2025 report](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf)).

## Is it really AI?

BACS is careful here. In shock calls, many people are sure they heard their own child. BACS says it is possible that scammers used voices cloned from social media videos. But it is also possible that, under stress, people simply believe they recognised the voice. BACS can’t confirm in each case whether AI was used ([BACS: Woche 31](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/26w31-de)).

Either way, the lesson is the same: **the voice is not proof.**

BACS also answers a common worry: it knows of no cases where a simple “hello” on a silent call was used to clone a whole voice. Silent calls are more often a test of whether someone answers ([BACS: Woche 31](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/26w31-de)).

## What to do

The FTC and BACS give the same advice ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/scammers-use-fake-emergencies-steal-your-money); [BACS: Tips for seniors](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/ecsm24-4-de)):

- **Don’t trust the voice**, even if it sounds exactly right.
- **Hang up**, or say “I’ll call you right back.”
- **Call back on the number you already have.**
- **Call another family member**, even if the caller asked you to keep it secret.
- **Ask a question only the real person would know**, or use your family code word (lesson 09.03).

:::practice call-drill scenario=voice

:::helper
Explain voice cloning to your relative calmly, without fear: “Someone could copy my voice. If I ever call asking for money, hang up and call me back. I won’t be offended.” Then agree on a code word together (lesson 09.03).
:::end
