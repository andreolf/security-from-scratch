:::story The perfect copy
Bruno is 72 and lives in Chur. He searches for his electricity company to pay a bill. He taps the first result. The page looks exactly right: the logo, the colours, the photos. It asks him to sign in.

Then Bruno looks at the address bar. The name is a little odd: two letters are swapped. He closes the page, finds the paper bill, and types the address printed on it. That is the real site. The first one was a copy.
:::end

:::remember
**A website can look perfect and still be fake. Check the address, and go in through your own bookmark or app.**
:::end

## Copies are easy to make

Anyone can copy the look of a website: the logo, the colours, the text. A fake site may look even more polished than the real one. So the look of a page tells you nothing about who runs it.

Fake sites reach people in many ways: through links in emails and texts, through QR codes, and through search adverts. The Swiss Federal Office for Cyber Security warns that a QR code can lead to a copy of a real website ([BACS: Phishing](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/phishing-de)), and reports that criminals use fraudulent adverts in search engines ([BACS half-year report](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/hjb-25-2-de)).

## Look-alike addresses

Scammers choose addresses that look almost right at a quick glance:

- a letter swapped: **twlnt.ch** instead of twint.ch (an “l” instead of an “i”)
- letters that look alike together: **rn** looks like **m**
- an extra dash or word: **twint-ch.com**, **twint.ch.secure-pay.info**

The FTC advises looking closely for misspellings or a switched letter ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/12/scammers-hide-harmful-links-qr-codes-steal-your-information)). Lesson 02.02 shows how to find the real website name.

## What the padlock really means

Many people learned: “Look for the padlock, then the site is safe.” That is no longer true.

:::figure padlock
The padlock means a private connection, not an honest website.
:::end

The padlock and “https” mean that the connection between your phone and the website is private, so others can’t read along. They don’t tell you who you are talking to. Fake sites can have a padlock too. The FBI warns: do not trust a website just because it has a lock icon or “https” in the address ([FBI IC3](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2019/PSA190610)).

:::helper
Many older adults were taught that the padlock means “safe”. Gently update that advice, and explain why it changed. Then show the alternative: a bookmark or the official app for every site where they sign in or pay. Lesson 02.04 shows how to set that up together.
:::end

:::quiz
- q: A website looks exactly like your bank’s site. Does that prove it is real?
  options: ["Yes, if the logo is right", "No, the look of a site is easy to copy", "Yes, if it loads quickly"]
  answer: 1
  explain: Logos and colours can be copied in minutes.
- q: What does the padlock next to a web address mean?
  options: ["The website is honest", "The connection is private, but it says nothing about who runs the site", "The website has been checked by the police"]
  answer: 1
  explain: The FBI warns not to trust a site just because it has a lock icon or https.
- q: Which of these addresses is the real TWINT website?
  options: ["twlnt.ch", "twint.ch", "twint-ch.com"]
  answer: 1
  explain: “twlnt” has an “l” instead of an “i”, and “twint-ch.com” is a different address.
:::end
