:::story Lotte’s three icons
Lotte is 79 and lives in Thun. Her granddaughter set up her phone with three icons on the first screen: her bank’s app, the TWINT app, and a bookmark to her health insurance website.

Now Lotte has one simple rule. When an email or text says “your bank needs you”, she never taps it. She taps her own bank icon instead. If there really is a message, it is waiting there. If not, the email was fake. “I don’t have to be a detective,” she says. “I just use my own door.”
:::end

:::remember
**Use your own door: a bookmark or the official app. Never the link in a message.**
:::end

## Why this works

Lessons 02.01 to 02.03 showed how hard it can be to spot a fake message or a fake website. This lesson gives you a way around the problem: **you don’t have to spot the fake at all**.

If you always open your bank, your shops and your important services in the same way, through a bookmark you saved or the official app, then a fake link never gets a chance. It doesn’t matter how real the email looks.

:::figure safe-way-in
Three ways in. Only your own bookmark or app is safe.
:::end

This is exactly what the US Federal Trade Commission recommends: if you think a message might be real, contact the company using a phone number or website you know is real, not the information in the message ([FTC: Phishing](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-recognize-and-avoid-phishing-scams)).

## Be careful with search too

Searching for your bank’s name and tapping the first result can also go wrong. The Swiss Federal Office for Cyber Security reports that criminals run fraudulent adverts in search engines ([BACS half-year report](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/hjb-25-2-de)). An advert can appear above the real result. Save the real address once, as a bookmark, and use that.

## Where to get the real address

Take it from something you can trust: your bank card, a paper letter or bill, or the official app. TWINT, for example, advises scanning TWINT QR codes only with the TWINT app ([TWINT: Sicher twinten](https://www.twint.ch/privatkunden/support/sicherheit/sicher-twinten/)).

:::try Save a bookmark for a site you use often
Type the address from a letter or bill yourself, check that the page is right, then save it.

--- iPhone
1. Open **Safari** and go to the website you want to save.
2. Tap the menu button, tap **Add Bookmark to**, choose a place, then tap **Save**. (If your tabs bar is at the top or bottom, touch and hold the bookmarks button, then tap **Add Bookmark**.)
3. To put an icon on your Home Screen instead: tap **Share**, scroll down, and tap **Add to Home Screen**, then **Add**.

The buttons look slightly different depending on your Safari layout. Apple’s page shows pictures. Checked against: [Apple Support: Bookmark a website in Safari](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/bookmark-a-website-iph42ab2f3a7/ios).

--- Android
1. Open **Chrome** and go to the website you want to save.
2. To the right of the address bar, tap **More** (three dots), then tap **Add to bookmarks**.
3. To open it later: tap **More**, then **Bookmarks**, then tap the bookmark.

Checked against: [Google Chrome Help: Create, find & edit bookmarks](https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/188842?co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid&hl=en).
:::end

:::exercise Your list of doors
Write down the three to five websites or apps where you sign in or pay: for example your bank, a shop, your health insurance, your email. Next to each one, write how you will open it from now on: “app” or “bookmark”.
:::solution
A typical list: bank (app), TWINT (app), health insurance (bookmark), online shop (bookmark), email (app). With a family helper, check each app came from the official app store and each bookmark was typed from a letter, not from a search result.
:::end

:::helper
Set up the “doors” together: install official apps from the app store (check the publisher’s name), and save bookmarks by typing the address from a letter. Put them on the first screen of the phone. Then agree the rule: “If a message says you need to do something, use your own door.”
:::end
