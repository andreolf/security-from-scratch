:::story The red screen
Heinz is 83 and lives in Winterthur. He is reading the weather forecast when the screen turns red and a voice shouts: “Your computer is infected! Call Microsoft Support immediately!” A Swiss phone number flashes on the screen. Heinz’s heart is racing.

He doesn’t call. He remembers his neighbour’s story about a “Microsoft” man who emptied her account. He switches the computer off at the button, waits a minute, and switches it on again. The red screen is gone. The weather forecast is still there.

*Heinz is made up. Screens like this are real.*
:::end

:::remember
**A real warning never shows a phone number. Don’t call. Close the browser or restart the computer.**
:::end

## How the trick works

While you browse, a website, or an advert on a normal website, opens a frightening page. It may fill the whole screen, refuse to close, and play a voice. It says your computer is infected or locked, and gives a number to call. The Swiss Federal Office for Cyber Security reports that these numbers are often Swiss, and that neither the browser nor the computer is actually locked ([BACS: Fake Support](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/24w41-de)).

If you call, a “support agent” pretends to be from Microsoft or Apple, claims to find viruses, and asks you to install a remote-control program. Then they lead you to a payment page or into your own e-banking ([cybercrimepolice.ch: Falsche Sicherheitswarnung](https://cybercrimepolice.ch/de/faelle/falsche-sicherheitswarnung-beim-surfen-im-internet)).

:::figure fake-virus
A loud warning with a phone number is the scam.
:::end

## Two facts that protect you

Microsoft writes that its error and warning messages never include a phone number, and that it doesn’t make unsolicited calls to fix your computer ([Microsoft Support: Protect yourself from tech support scams](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/protect-yourself-from-tech-support-scams)).

The US Federal Trade Commission gives the same two rules: legitimate tech companies won’t contact you to say there is a problem with your computer, and real security warnings never ask you to call a phone number ([FTC: Tech support scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-spot-avoid-and-report-tech-support-scams)).

It is a big business. In 2025, people aged 60 and over reported 21,333 tech-support scams to the FBI, with losses of over $1 billion ([FBI IC3 Internet Crime Report 2025](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf)).

## What to do when it happens

1. **Don’t call** the number, and don’t click anything on the page.
2. **Close the browser.** If it won’t close: on Windows, press Ctrl + Alt + Delete, open Task Manager and end the browser; on a Mac, press Cmd + Option + Esc and force quit the browser ([cybercrimepolice.ch](https://cybercrimepolice.ch/de/faelle/falsche-sicherheitswarnung-beim-surfen-im-internet)).
3. **Or restart the computer.** BACS says closing the browser or restarting usually solves it ([BACS](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/24w41-de)).

If you already called and let someone in, see lesson 05.04: call your bank, change your passwords, and have the computer checked.
