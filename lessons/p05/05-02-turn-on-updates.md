:::story One evening, four devices
Pierre is 75 and lives in Fribourg. He has an iPhone, his wife Anne has an Android phone, and they share a Windows laptop. Their daughter visits with a plan: “Let’s set every device to update by itself. Then we never have to think about it again.”

It takes about half an hour, with a cup of tea. Most of it is waiting for the first update to download.

*Pierre and Anne are made up.*
:::end

:::remember
**Turn on automatic updates once, on every device. Then plug in and use Wi-Fi at night, and the device does the rest.**
:::end

## Before you start

Updates can be large. Android’s help page suggests connecting to Wi-Fi and charging your device to at least 75% first ([Android Help](https://support.google.com/android/answer/7680439?hl=en)). Apple suggests a backup first, then plugging into power and using Wi-Fi ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118575)).

:::try Turn on automatic updates
Pick your device. Menu names can differ slightly between models and versions.

--- iPhone
1. Open **Settings**, then tap **General**.
2. Tap **Software Update**, then **Automatic Updates**.
3. Turn on **Automatically Install** (and **Automatically Download**). Your iPhone then installs updates overnight while charging and connected to Wi-Fi, and tells you first.

Apps from the App Store update automatically by default. Checked against: [Apple Support: Update your iPhone or iPad](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118575) and [Apple Support: Update apps](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102629).

--- Android
1. Most system updates and security fixes install automatically. When you see an update notification, open it and tap the update button.
2. To check yourself: open **Settings**, tap **System**, then **Software updates** (on Pixel phones; other makers use similar names).
3. For apps: open **Google Play**, tap your **profile picture**, then **Settings**, **Network Preferences**, **Auto-update apps**, and choose an option such as **Over Wi-Fi only**.

Checked against: [Android Help: Check & update your Android version](https://support.google.com/android/answer/7680439?hl=en) and [Google Play Help: How to update apps](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/113412?hl=en).

--- Windows
1. Select **Start**, then **Settings**, then **Windows Update**.
2. Select **Check for Windows updates**. If updates are available, select **Download & install**.
3. Restart when asked.

Checked against: [Microsoft Support: Install Windows Updates](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/deployment/updates-lifecycle/install-windows-updates).

--- Mac
1. Choose the **Apple menu**, then **System Settings**.
2. Click **General**, then **Software Update**.
3. Install any update shown. In the Software Update settings there, choose whether your Mac downloads and installs updates automatically.

Checked against: [Apple Support: Update macOS on Mac](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108382).
:::end

## Don’t forget the apps

Your phone’s system is one part. Each app also gets fixes. Google notes that updating your apps improves app security ([Google Play Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/113412?hl=en)). On iPhone, App Store apps update by themselves unless this was switched off ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102629)).

:::exercise Your update check
For each device in your home (phone, tablet, computer), write down: is automatic updating on? When was the last update?
:::solution
If every device says “up to date” and automatic updates are on, you are done. If a device can no longer get updates because it is too old, use it only for things that don’t involve money or passwords, or plan to replace it. Smart TVs and routers have updates too; your helper can check those.
:::end

:::helper
Do one device at a time, and let your relative tap. The first update may take a while; start it in the evening and let it run overnight on the charger. Write the date on the trusted-numbers card: “Updates checked on …”.
:::end
