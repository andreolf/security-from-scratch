:::story The parking app
Marco is 71 and lives in Lugano. In a car park, a sticker on the ticket machine says: “Pay by app! Scan here.” The QR code opens a website that offers to download a parking app straight from the page, “not in the store yet”.

Marco hesitates. He remembers his daughter’s rule: “Apps only from the store, and only ones you went looking for.” He pays with coins instead. At home he searches the App Store for the city’s parking app and finds the real one, made by the city’s official partner.

*Marco is made up.*
:::end

:::remember
**Apps only from the App Store or Google Play, only ones you chose yourself, and check the maker’s name.**
:::end

## Why the official store matters

An app can do a lot on your phone: read messages, see your screen, use your banking. So where it comes from matters. The Swiss Federal Office for Cyber Security recommends downloading apps only from official app stores, and checking ratings and the access the app asks for ([BACS: Geräteschutz](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/geraeteschutz)).

The stores check apps before they appear. Apple says it reviews apps to check that they come from known sources and are free of known malware ([Apple: App Store](https://www.apple.com/app-store/)). Google Play Protect runs a safety check on apps from Google Play before you download them, and also checks your phone for harmful apps from other sources ([Google Play Help: Play Protect](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/2812853?hl=en)).

## Copycats exist, even in the store

The checks are good, but not perfect. A fake app may copy a bank’s or a shop’s logo. Before installing, look at the **maker’s name** under the app’s title. If you’re unsure, go to the company’s own website (through your bookmark) and follow its link to the store.

## The dangerous moment: “install this for me”

Most harmful installs don’t start in the store. They start with a person: a caller from the “bank”, a “parcel” text, a pop-up. If anyone who contacted you asks you to install an app, the answer is no. Microsoft gives the same advice for computers: download software only from official sites or the Microsoft Store ([Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/protect-yourself-from-tech-support-scams)).

:::try Check that Play Protect is on
These steps are for Android phones. Play Protect is part of Google Play.

--- Android
1. Open the **Google Play Store** app.
2. At the top right, tap your **profile icon**.
3. Tap **Play Protect**, then **Settings**.
4. Check that **Scan apps with Play Protect** is on. Google recommends always keeping it on.

Checked against: [Google Play Help: Use Google Play Protect](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/2812853?hl=en).
:::end

:::helper
Scroll through the apps on your relative’s phone together. Remove ones they don’t recognise or no longer use, especially remote-control apps. If they want a new app, they can call you first; together you find it in the store and check the maker.
:::end
