:::story “Remind me later”
Ursula is 79 and lives in St. Gallen. For months her tablet has shown the same message: “An update is available.” She always taps “Later”. “It works fine as it is. What if the update changes everything?”

Her grandson explains it like a house. “The builders found a window that doesn’t lock properly. The update is them coming to fix it. ‘Later’ means the window stays open.” Ursula plugs in the tablet that evening and lets it update overnight.

*Ursula is made up.*
:::end

:::remember
**An update fixes holes that criminals can use. Say yes to updates, or better: turn on automatic updates.**
:::end

## What an update does

Every app and every phone has small mistakes in its programming. Some of them are security holes: ways for a criminal to get in. The makers keep finding and fixing these holes, and an update delivers the fix to your device.

The Swiss Federal Office for Cyber Security explains that all software contains such weaknesses, and that criminals use them to take control of devices, plant harmful programs and steal login or card details ([BACS: Carry out regular updates](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/en/ks24-1-en)).

:::figure update-holes
An update closes the holes.
:::end

## Why “later” is risky

Once a fix is published, the hole is known, also to criminals. Devices that haven’t updated are the easy targets. CISA, the US cyber security agency, says it plainly: many software updates are created to fix security risks, so think twice before tapping “Remind me later” ([CISA: Update software](https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world/update-software)).

Apple calls keeping your software up to date one of the most important things you can do for your device’s security ([Apple Support: Update your iPhone or iPad](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118575)).

## The easy way: automatic updates

You don’t need to remember anything. Turn on automatic updates once, and your phone or computer installs them by itself, often at night while charging. BACS recommends switching on automatic updates for the operating system and for all your apps ([BACS](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/en/ks24-1-en)). The next lesson shows how.

## Real updates come from the device itself

A real update appears in your phone’s or computer’s **Settings**, or in the official app store. A website or a message that says “Your phone needs an urgent update, tap here” is a trick. Close it, and check in Settings yourself.

:::exercise Where do real updates come from?
Which of these is a real update? (a) A pop-up on a news website: “Your Android is out of date! Download the update here.” (b) A message from your phone’s Settings: “Software update available.”
:::solution
(b) is real: it comes from your phone itself. (a) is a trick: websites cannot see whether your phone is up to date, and real updates never come as downloads from a website. Close it.
:::end

:::helper
Check the date of the last update together (in Settings, under Software Update or System). If the phone is very old, it may no longer receive updates; that is worth a calm conversation about a newer second-hand phone.
:::end
