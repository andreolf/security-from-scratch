:::story The helpful IT specialist
Silvia is 76 and lives in Aarau. A woman calls from “her bank”: a wrong payment has been booked on her account, and she will connect Silvia to the bank’s IT specialist. A calm man explains that Silvia’s e-banking is infected. “To protect your money, please install a small support app. Then I can clean everything.”

Silvia says she’ll call back, and hangs up. She calls her bank on the number on her card. There was no wrong payment, and nobody from the bank had called.

*Silvia is made up. The Swiss Federal Office for Cyber Security describes calls exactly like this.*
:::end

:::remember
**Never install an app because a caller asked, and never let someone who contacted you control your screen. Hang up and call your bank yourself.**
:::end

## What remote control means

[[term:remote-access|Remote-control]] apps, such as AnyDesk or TeamViewer, are real tools. IT helpers use them to fix computers from far away. But once someone has that access, they can see your screen, move your mouse and type, as if they were sitting at your desk.

In April 2026, the Swiss Federal Office for Cyber Security reported a rise in calls from fake bank staff who pass the victim to an “IT specialist”. The specialist pushes the victim to install AnyDesk, TeamViewer or a similar program. With that access, criminals can see the screen, control the mouse, steal passwords and get into e-banking ([BACS: Woche 15](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/26w15-de)).

## The rule

BACS is clear: banks, credit card companies and authorities will never ask you on the phone to install remote-maintenance software. End such calls immediately ([BACS](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/26w15-de)). The same goes for “Microsoft” or “Apple” callers ([Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/protect-yourself-from-tech-support-scams)).

Only use remote help when **you** asked a person **you** know, such as your family helper, and you called them on a number you already had.

:::practice call-drill scenario=support

## If it already happened

Don’t be ashamed: these callers are professionals. Act quickly, in this order ([BACS](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/26w15-de), [cybercrimepolice.ch](https://cybercrimepolice.ch/de/faelle/falsche-sicherheitswarnung-beim-surfen-im-internet)):

1. **Call your bank** on the number on your card, and have your card and e-banking access blocked if you gave any details.
2. **Remove the app**, and change all passwords you use on that device. Turn on two-step sign-in where you can.
3. **Have the device checked** by a specialist, or reset and set up again.
4. **Report it** to the police, especially if money was lost.

:::helper
If you help your relative remotely, agree a rule: you will only ever connect when **they** called **you**. Never on a call that starts with you. Write that on the trusted-numbers card. Check which remote apps are installed on their devices, and remove any they don’t know.
:::end
