:::story The lawyer from Geneva
Hans is 77 and lives in Winterthur. Six months after losing CHF 30,000 to a fake trading app, he gets an email from a “victims’ lawyer in Geneva”. They say they have traced his money and can return it, after a CHF 2,000 “administration fee”.

Hans wants to believe it. But he remembers the police officer saying: “They often come back.” He calls the police on the number he finds himself. They confirm: it is the same kind of scam, a second time.

*Hans is made up.*
:::end

:::remember
**Anyone who wants a fee to get your money back is a scammer. Hang up, and check with the police on a number you find yourself.**
:::end

## Why scammers come back

The Swiss financial regulator FINMA warns that lists of defrauded people circulate and are sold to new scammers, who offer to recover the losses. Victims usually lose a second time, paying new money and fees ([FINMA: Beispiele für Anlagebetrug](https://www.finma.ch/de/finma-public/wie-sie-sich-schuetzen-koennen/beispiele-fuer-anlagebetrug/)). The US Federal Trade Commission describes the same “refund and recovery” scams: the scammers pretend to be a government agency, a consumer group or a law firm, and ask for an upfront retainer, processing fee or tax ([FTC: Refund and recovery scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/refund-and-recovery-scams)).

Swiss Crime Prevention warns that after a romance scam, a fake “authority” or “lawyer” may offer to return the money for a fee. Don’t react to such supposed helpers, whether they claim to be Interpol, foreign police or victims’ lawyers ([SKP: Romance Scam](https://www.skppsc.ch/de/themen/internet/romance-scam/)).

## What real agencies say

- The FTC: government agencies and legitimate organisations will never ask for money to help you get a refund, and won’t guarantee you’ll get it back ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/refund-and-recovery-scams)).
- FINMA generally doesn’t contact private people, and is never involved in offers to recover losses ([FINMA](https://www.finma.ch/de/finma-public/wie-sie-sich-schuetzen-koennen/beispiele-fuer-anlagebetrug/)).
- The FBI’s IC3 says it does not work with law firms or crypto services to recover lost money, and will never contact you directly for information or money ([IC3](https://www.ic3.gov/)).

## What to do

1. Don’t pay anything, and don’t give bank details or remote access.
2. Hang up or don’t reply.
3. If you want to check, call the police or agency on a number you found yourself (lesson 00.04).
4. Report the contact. It is a new crime (lesson 10.03).

:::end
