:::story Two reports, ten minutes
Marco is 69 and lives in Lugano. He paid CHF 300 for a garden bench that never came. He thinks: “It’s too little to bother the police.”

His daughter disagrees. They phone the police station first, then file the complaint. At home they also send a short report to the federal cyber security office. A month later, the police tell Marco that the same fake shop had cheated dozens of people, and his report helped close it.

*Marco is made up.*
:::end

:::remember
**Report every scam, even small ones. Keep all evidence. Contacts below were checked on 26 October 2026.**
:::end

## Why report?

The Swiss Federal Office for Cyber Security (BACS) recommends filing a criminal complaint with your local police, ideally after phoning ahead. Even when the money went abroad or into crypto and is hard to get back, reporting is still important ([BACS: Finanzieller Schaden](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/finanzieller-schaden)). Swiss Crime Prevention adds that your report can protect others ([SKP: Romance Scam](https://www.skppsc.ch/de/themen/internet/romance-scam/)).

Bring what you kept: messages, phone numbers, website addresses, account numbers, receipts and dates.

:::figure report-where
Where to report, in each region.
:::end

## Switzerland

- **Emergency:** call 117 (police) or 112. The federal reporting site says reports there are not handled around the clock, so in an acute emergency call 112 or 117 ([report.ncsc.admin.ch](https://www.report.ncsc.admin.ch/en/)).
- **Police complaint:** at your local police station. You can find stations on Suisse ePolice, suisse-epolice.ch ([BACS: Romance Scam](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/romance-scam-de)). For a few listed offences, such as an online purchase that never arrived, you can file the complaint online ([report.ncsc.admin.ch](https://www.report.ncsc.admin.ch/en/)).
- **Also tell BACS:** on the federal reporting form you answer a few questions and get an automatic first assessment with what to do next. This helps BACS warn others. BACS itself does not take criminal complaints, so still go to the police ([report.ncsc.admin.ch](https://www.report.ncsc.admin.ch/en/)).

## European Union

Each country has its own way. Europol links to the reporting website of every EU country, and says that where there is no online option, you go to your local police station ([Europol: Report cybercrime online](https://www.europol.europa.eu/report-a-crime/report-cybercrime-online)). In an emergency, call 112 anywhere in the EU ([European Commission: 112](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/112)).

## United States

- Report the scam to the Federal Trade Commission at ReportFraud.ftc.gov ([FTC: What to do if you were scammed](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-do-if-you-were-scammed)).
- Report internet crime to the FBI’s Internet Crime Complaint Center at ic3.gov, the FBI’s central hub for cyber-enabled crime ([IC3](https://www.ic3.gov/)).
- If you are 60 or older, the Justice Department’s National Elder Fraud Hotline is 1-833-FRAUD-11 (833-372-8311), 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. Eastern Time, Monday to Friday ([DOJ Elder Justice](https://www.justice.gov/elderjustice/find-help-or-report-abuse)). In an emergency call 911.
- If your Social Security number was given or misused, IdentityTheft.gov gives a personal recovery plan ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-do-if-you-were-scammed)).

:::exercise Write your own reporting card
On the back of your trusted-numbers card, write: your local police station’s number, the emergency number (117 or 112 in Switzerland, 112 in the EU, 911 in the US), and the reporting website for where you live from the list above.
:::solution
Find your local police number on your canton’s or city’s official police website, or on Suisse ePolice. Keep the card with your bank’s block number. Then everything you need after a scam is in one place.
:::end

:::helper
Offer to go with your relative to the police station, or sit with them while they fill in the online form. Many people put off reporting because they feel ashamed; your company makes it easier.
:::end

:::quiz

  options: ["No, too small", "Yes. Every report helps, and can protect others", "Only above CHF 1,000"]
  answer: 1

- q: Does BACS take criminal complaints?
  options: ["Yes", "No. Report to BACS to help warn others, and go to the police for a complaint", "Only by fax"]
  answer: 1
  explain: The federal reporting site says BACS does not accept criminal complaints.
- q: Where do people in the US report a scam?
  options: ["ReportFraud.ftc.gov and ic3.gov", "On social media", "To the scammer’s bank"]
  answer: 0
  explain: The FTC and the FBI’s IC3 collect scam reports.
:::end
