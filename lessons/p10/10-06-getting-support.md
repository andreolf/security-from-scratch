:::story Telling the book club
Anna is 74 and lives in Zug. After a romance scam she stops going to her book club. She can’t sleep and feels foolish.

One night she calls 143 and talks for half an hour. The next week she tells her friend Vreni. Vreni says: “My brother lost money the same way.” Anna goes back to the book club and, later, tells the group what happened, so they know the signs.

*Anna is made up.*
:::end

:::remember
**You don’t have to carry this alone. Talk to someone you trust, or call a free support line.**
:::end

## It is normal to feel bad

Losing money to a scam can bring shame, anger, sadness and sleepless nights, especially after a romance scam, where the “relationship” felt real. None of this means you are weak. Swiss Crime Prevention advises: talk to a person you trust, and seek psychological help if you need it ([SKP: Romance Scam](https://www.skppsc.ch/de/themen/internet/romance-scam/)). The Swiss Federal Office for Cyber Security says the same: if the experience weighs on you, talk to someone you trust ([BACS: Romance Scam](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/romance-scam-de)).

## Free support lines

Checked on 26 October 2026:

- **Switzerland:** Die Dargebotene Hand answers on **143**, personally, anonymously and confidentially, with chat and email too. Support in English is on 0800 143 000 from 6 to 11 pm ([143.ch](https://www.143.ch/)).
- **European Union:** 116 006 is the European helpline for victims of crime, with trained staff giving information and support, anonymously and confidentially. **Availability varies by country**: in 2022, the Commission said thirteen EU countries had set it up ([European Commission](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/just/items/737172/en)). Try the number, or ask your police for local victim support.
- **United States:** the AARP Fraud Watch Network Helpline is 877-908-3360, Monday to Friday 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. Eastern Time. It gives guidance and support, not investigation, and runs free online support groups ([AARP](https://www.aarp.org/money/scams-fraud/helpline/)). For people 60 or older, the National Elder Fraud Hotline is 1-833-FRAUD-11 ([DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/elderjustice/find-help-or-report-abuse)).

## If money is now a problem

BACS advises contacting your canton’s debt advice service if the scam has left you with money problems ([BACS: Romance Scam](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/romance-scam-de)).

## Why talking helps others

Scams thrive on silence. When you tell friends what happened, they learn the signs. Swiss Crime Prevention notes that your report can protect other victims ([SKP](https://www.skppsc.ch/de/themen/internet/romance-scam/)). You decide how much to share, and when.

:::exercise Who would you call?
Write down one person you would tell if you were scammed, and one support line from the list above for where you live. Add both to your trusted-numbers card.
:::solution
There is no wrong answer. Pick someone who listens without blaming. If no one comes to mind, the support line is there for exactly this, day or night in Switzerland.
:::end

:::helper
If someone tells you they were scammed, listen first. Don’t say “How could you?”. Say: “Thank you for telling me. It’s not your fault.” Then offer help with the steps in this phase.
:::end
