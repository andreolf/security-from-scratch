:::story The safe phone
Elena is 72 and lives in Basel. A fake “Microsoft” caller had control of her laptop for an hour. Her nephew says: “Don’t touch the laptop for now. Let’s use your phone.”

On the phone they change her email password, then her Apple Account password, and remove a computer she doesn’t recognise. Then they change her bank password. The laptop goes to a repair shop to be checked.

*Elena is made up.*
:::end

:::remember
**Email first. Use a device the scammer never touched. New password, two-step sign-in, remove unknown devices.**
:::end

## Why email comes first

Your email is the key to your other accounts: “forgot password” links are sent there (lesson 03.05). So secure it first.

The US Federal Trade Commission says: if you gave a scammer your password, create a new, strong password, change it on any other account where you used it, and turn on two-factor authentication. If you gave a scammer remote access to your computer, update your security software, run a scan, and change your passwords and turn on two-factor authentication ([FTC: What to do if you were scammed](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-do-if-you-were-scammed)).

If the scammer had your computer, change passwords from another device you trust, such as your phone.

## Signs your account was taken

Apple lists signs such as: a notice about a sign-in you don’t recognise, a two-factor code you didn’t ask for, or a password that no longer works ([Apple: If you think your Apple Account has been compromised](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102560)). Google says you can review recent security events and devices in your Google Account, and choose “No, it wasn’t me” for anything you don’t recognise ([Google: Secure a hacked Google Account](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/6294825?hl=en)).

## Change your phone account password

:::try Change your password
--- iPhone
1. Open the **Settings** app and tap **your name**.
2. Tap **Sign-In & Security**.
3. Tap **Change Password** and follow the steps on the screen.
4. You can choose **Remove Other Devices**, so every other device has to sign in again.

Steps from [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/101567). Apple notes you may have to wait an hour if Stolen Device Protection is on.
--- Android
1. Open the **Settings** app.
2. Tap **Google**, then **your name**, then **Manage your Google Account**.
3. Tap **Security & sign-in**.
4. Under “How you sign in to Google”, tap **Password**, enter the new password and tap **Change Password**.

Steps from [Google Account Help](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/41078?co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid&hl=en).
:::end

## If you can’t sign in any more

- Apple: go to iforgot.apple.com to start account recovery, and at account.apple.com remove devices you don’t recognise ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102560)).
- Google: use the Google account recovery page and answer the questions as best you can ([Google Account Help](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/6294825?hl=en)).
- Other email providers: use the “forgot password” link on the provider’s own website, typed in yourself.

Then change your bank and shopping passwords, one by one. A password manager helps (lesson 03.03).

:::exercise Your email check, today
Even if nothing has happened, sign in to your email account settings and look at the list of devices signed in. Do you recognise each one? Is two-step sign-in on?
:::solution
If you see a device you don’t know, remove it and change your password. If two-step sign-in is off, turn it on (lesson 04.03 shows how). Doing this on a calm day makes it easy on a bad one.
:::end

:::helper
Sit with your relative and do it together, but let them type. Write the new email password on paper and keep it somewhere safe at home, not in the phone notes.
:::end
