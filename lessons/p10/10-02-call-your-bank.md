:::story The 24-hour line
Kurt is 80 and lives in St. Gallen. At 10 pm he realises the “courier” website took his card details. He thinks: “The bank is closed, I’ll call tomorrow.”

Then he remembers the sticker his granddaughter put on his fridge: “Card blocked? Call this number, 24 hours.” He calls. Within minutes the card is blocked. The next morning, the bank tells him two payments were stopped.

*Kurt is made up.*
:::end

:::remember
**Call your bank or card company now, on the number on your card. Block, stop, and ask about reversing the payment.**
:::end

## Why the bank comes first

The Swiss Federal Office for Cyber Security (BACS) says to contact your bank or card provider immediately and have the affected accounts or cards blocked. Many providers have a 24-hour block hotline. Ask the bank to stop the fraudulent payment, have suspicious transactions checked and, where possible, reversed. The faster you act, the better the chances ([BACS: Finanzieller Schaden](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/finanzieller-schaden)).

For TWINT, BACS warns that payments are made immediately and usually can’t be reversed ([BACS](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/finanzieller-schaden)), and TWINT itself says to call your bank immediately if you were scammed ([TWINT: Sicher twinten](https://www.twint.ch/privatkunden/support/sicherheit/sicher-twinten/)).

## Depending on how you paid

The US Federal Trade Commission gives steps for each way of paying ([FTC: What to do if you were scammed](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-do-if-you-were-scammed)):

- **Bank transfer:** report it to your bank immediately and ask them to reverse the payment.
- **Credit or debit card:** report it to the card issuer immediately, using the number on the back of your card, and ask for a refund.
- **Gift card:** contact the gift card company immediately, using the number on the back of the card. Keep the card and the receipt.
- **Cryptocurrency:** contact the exchange or crypto ATM operator immediately and ask them to reverse it.

BACS adds that for payments abroad or in cryptocurrency, the chances of getting money back are usually much lower, but reporting is still important ([BACS](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/finanzieller-schaden)).

## Use the right number

Use the number printed on your card, on your bank statement or on your bank’s own website. Never use a number from the scam message or call (lesson 00.04).

:::exercise Find your block number now
Take out your bank card. Find the phone number on the back. Is it the bank’s 24-hour number for blocking cards? Write it on your trusted-numbers card, and save it in your phone as “Bank – block card”.
:::solution
If your card has no number, look on your bank’s official website or app for “block card” and write it down. Do the same for every card you have. In an emergency, you won’t want to search.
:::end

:::helper
Put your relative’s bank block number on a sticker near their phone, as in Kurt’s story. Make sure they know they can call at any time, day or night.
:::end
