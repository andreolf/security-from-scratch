:::story The afternoon Rita realised
Rita is 75 and lives in Thun. An hour after the call from “her bank”, she realises she has been tricked into moving CHF 4,000. She feels sick and ashamed and wants to hide.

Instead she takes a deep breath and opens her trusted-numbers card. She calls her bank’s number on the back of her card. The bank blocks her cards and tries to stop the payment. Then she calls her son, who comes over. Together they go through the next steps on this page.

*Rita is made up.*
:::end

:::remember
**It is not your fault. Act fast and in order: 1. stop contact, 2. call your bank, 3. report it, 4. secure your email.**
:::end

## First: it is not your fault

Scammers are organised and practised, and they use feelings, not stupidity (lesson 08.02). Swiss Crime Prevention says plainly: don’t blame yourself. The police don’t punish human weakness, they hunt criminals, and your report can protect others ([SKP: Romance Scam](https://www.skppsc.ch/de/themen/internet/romance-scam/)).

Feeling shaken is normal. Ask someone you trust to sit with you while you make the calls.

## Time matters

The Swiss Federal Office for Cyber Security (BACS) advises acting quickly and systematically: contact your bank or card company at once, and have accounts or cards blocked. The faster you act, the better the chances ([BACS: Finanzieller Schaden](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/finanzieller-schaden)). The US Federal Trade Commission also says: the sooner you act, the better, and it is always worth asking the company you used to send money whether it can be reversed ([FTC: What to do if you were scammed](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-do-if-you-were-scammed)).

## The four steps

1. **Stop all contact.** End the call, don’t reply, don’t send anything more. Keep all messages, numbers and receipts ([cybercrimepolice.ch](https://cybercrimepolice.ch/de/faelle/liebesbetrug)).
2. **Call your bank** or card company, on the number on your card. Lesson 10.02.
3. **Report it** to the police, and to the right office where you live. Lesson 10.03.
4. **Secure your accounts**, starting with your email, from a device you trust. Lesson 10.04.

Then watch out for a second scam: people who call offering to get your money back (lesson 10.05). And look after yourself (lesson 10.06).

:::practice scenes scene=firststeps

:::helper
If someone calls you in a panic after a scam, keep calm for them. Say: “It’s not your fault. Let’s call your bank first, right now.” Then help with the steps one at a time. Don’t take over their accounts without asking.
:::end
