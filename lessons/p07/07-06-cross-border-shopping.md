:::story The walking boots
Rosa is 68 and lives in Basel. She orders walking boots from a shop abroad because they are cheaper. Three weeks later nothing has arrived, and the shop doesn’t answer.

Rosa had kept the order confirmation and the payment. She writes to the shop once more, then contacts her card company about the charge. When that takes time, she fills in the online form of the Ombudsstelle E-Commerce, the free complaints office for online purchases in Switzerland. It explains her next steps.

*Rosa is made up.*
:::end

:::remember
**Before you order from abroad, check who the seller is and what it will really cost. If something goes wrong: keep records, write to the shop, then ask the right office for help.**
:::end

## Before you order

- **Know who you’re buying from.** SECO, the Swiss State Secretariat for Economic Affairs, notes that a .ch address does not prove a Swiss company, so check the seller’s identity in the legal notice. Only addresses ending in .swiss are certain to belong to Swiss companies or citizens ([SECO: Einkaufen im Ausland](https://www.seco.admin.ch/de/einkaufen-im-ausland)).
- **Count the full price.** Goods from abroad generally owe VAT and customs, which the carrier bills to you ([SECO](https://www.seco.admin.ch/de/einkaufen-im-ausland)). The US Federal Trade Commission also advises working out the total cost, including shipping, taxes and other fees ([FTC: Online Shopping](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/online-shopping)).
- **Beware of brand bargains.** The FTC warns that expensive brand-name items at bargain prices could be counterfeit or stolen ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/online-shopping)). SECO adds that fakes can be seized at the border, even if you didn’t know they were fake ([SECO](https://www.seco.admin.ch/de/einkaufen-im-ausland)).
- **Keep records.** Save the shop’s name and website, what you ordered and paid, the return policy and every message ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/online-shopping)).

## If something goes wrong

**First, the shop.** Write to the seller and describe the problem calmly. The FTC advises trying to work it out with the seller first ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/online-shopping)).

**Then, your card company.** If you paid by credit card and nothing came, contact your card company quickly. In the US, the FTC explains you can dispute the charge, and your letter must reach the card company within 60 days of the first bill showing the error ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/online-shopping)). Elsewhere, ask your card company about its rules.

**Then, the right office for where you live:**

- **Switzerland.** The Ombudsstelle E-Commerce, run by the Konsumentenforum in Bern, helps free of charge after an online purchase when you couldn’t solve the problem with the company. Requests go through its online form. It can also be reached on 031 380 50 34, Monday to Friday 09:00 to 12:00 ([Ombudsstelle E-Commerce](https://konsum.ch/de/ombudsstellen/ombudsstelle-e-commerce/); [SECO: Wo finde ich Hilfe?](https://www.seco.admin.ch/de/wo-finde-ich-hilfe)). The Federal Consumer Affairs Bureau answers general questions on +41 58 462 20 00, Monday, Wednesday and Friday 10:00 to 11:00, but gives no legal advice ([BFK: Konsumenten-Helpline](https://www.konsum.admin.ch/de/konsumenten-helpline)).
- **EU, Iceland or Norway.** If you live there, the European Consumer Centre in your country can explain your rights and help with a dispute with a seller in another EU country, Iceland or Norway ([European Commission: ECC-Net](https://commission.europa.eu/topics/consumers/consumer-rights-and-complaints/resolve-your-consumer-complaint/european-consumer-centres-network-ecc-net_en)). Switzerland is not on that list, so people living in Switzerland should use the Swiss offices above.
- **United States.** If you can’t work it out with the seller, tell the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/online-shopping)).

SECO adds that if no solution is found, consumers in Switzerland can usually go to court where they live, in a simplified, low-cost procedure that starts with an attempt at settlement ([SECO: Wo finde ich Hilfe?](https://www.seco.admin.ch/de/wo-finde-ich-hilfe)).

*Phone numbers and hours checked on 26 October 2026. Check the office’s website before you call, as they can change.*

:::exercise Your records folder
Think of your last online order from abroad. Could you find these within five minutes?

1. The shop’s name and web address
2. The order confirmation and the price
3. How you paid, and the line on your statement
4. Any messages with the shop
:::solution
If any are missing, make a folder now, on paper or on your computer, called “Online orders”. Save each order confirmation in it until the item has arrived and the return period is over. If something goes wrong, everything an office will ask for is in one place.
:::end

:::helper
If a relative orders a lot from abroad, help them set up the folder. Note the Ombudsstelle E-Commerce (in Switzerland) or the right office where they live on their trusted-numbers card (lesson 00.04).
:::end
