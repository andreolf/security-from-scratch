:::story The confirmation Hans didn’t start
Hans is 74 and lives in Biel. He is reading the newspaper when his phone shows a message from his card company’s app: “Please confirm your payment of CHF 489.00 to ElectroShop24.” Hans hasn’t bought anything.

He taps **Decline**. Then he turns the card over and calls the number on the back. The card company blocks the card and sends him a new one. Someone had his card number, but without his confirmation the payment did not go through.

*Hans is made up.*
:::end

:::remember
**Confirm a card payment only if you started it yourself, with the right shop and the right amount. If not, decline and call the number on your card.**
:::end

## Which way to pay

How you pay decides how easy it is to get your money back if something goes wrong.

- **Invoice.** BACS advises preferring payment by invoice over paying in advance, when the shop offers it ([BACS: Sicher online unterwegs](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/sicher-online-unterwegs)).
- **Credit card.** The US Federal Trade Commission says paying by credit card best protects you if something goes wrong. In the US, if you’re billed for something you never got, you can dispute the charge, but you must act fast: your letter must reach the card company within 60 days of the first bill showing the error ([FTC: Online Shopping](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/online-shopping)). Rules elsewhere differ, so ask your own card company what applies to you.
- **Bank transfer in advance.** Avoid it with shops you don’t know. Scammers prefer payments that are hard to get back, such as wire transfers, gift cards and cryptocurrency ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/online-shopping)).

BACS also advises giving your card number only where you have to, and only on encrypted pages, the ones with “https” ([BACS](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/sicher-online-unterwegs)).

## The extra confirmation step

Many card payments online now need a second confirmation, a kind of [[term:two-factor|two-step check]] for payments. It is called 3-D Secure, or by brand names like “Mastercard Identity Check” and “Visa Secure”. Swisscard, for example, explains that after you pay online by credit card, you get an SMS code or a notification in its app to confirm the payment ([Swisscard: 3-D Secure](https://www.swisscard.ch/de/privatkunden/services/3d-secure)).

This step protects you only if you use it well:

- **Read it.** Check the shop’s name and the amount before you confirm.
- **Only your own purchases.** If a confirmation arrives and you aren’t buying anything, decline it.
- **Never for someone else.** A caller who asks you to confirm a payment “to stop a fraud” or “to get a refund” is the fraud. The code and the confirmation are for you alone (lesson 04.02).

## Check your statement

Once a month, read your card statement line by line. BACS advises checking your card statements and telling your card issuer about anything you don’t recognise ([BACS](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/sicher-online-unterwegs)). Use the phone number printed on your card, not one from a message.

:::exercise Read a statement
Take your most recent card statement, on paper or in your bank’s app. Go through each line and ask: “Do I know this shop, and this amount?” Mark anything you don’t recognise.
:::solution
If you find a payment you don’t recognise, call the number on the back of your card. Small unknown amounts matter too. If everything is familiar, well done: this monthly habit takes ten minutes.
:::end

:::helper
Help a relative set up payment notifications in their banking or card app, so they see every payment as it happens. Write the card company’s phone number on their trusted-numbers card (lesson 00.04).
:::end
