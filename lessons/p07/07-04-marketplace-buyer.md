:::story The armchair
Werner is 73 and lives in Schaffhausen. He puts his old armchair on an online marketplace for CHF 120. Ten minutes later a buyer writes: “I’ll take it! I’m abroad, my courier will collect it. Let’s chat on WhatsApp.”

On WhatsApp the buyer sends a link: “I’ve paid through the delivery service. Click and confirm with your card to receive the money.” The page looks like a real payment service and asks for Werner’s card number and a code.

Werner stops. Why would *he* need his card to *receive* money? He opens his own banking app: no money has arrived. He blocks the number and reports the buyer to the marketplace.

*Werner is made up. This trick is real.*
:::end

:::remember
**To receive money, you never need to enter card details or confirm a code. The only proof of payment is money arriving in your own account.**
:::end

## How the trick works

The Swiss Federal Office for Cyber Security (BACS) describes it step by step ([BACS: Woche 40](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/25w40-de)):

1. A “buyer” contacts you about something you are selling, and quickly moves the chat to WhatsApp, often from a Swiss mobile number.
2. They send a link or a QR code to a fake page of PostFinance or a payment or delivery service. It claims you will get the money if you “confirm” there.
3. The page asks for your card details or your e-banking login. With those, the criminals take money from you.

A newer twist: the buyer sends a file called something like “Twint-Rechnung.zip” and pushes you to open it on your computer. It contains harmful software. BACS’s report for the second half of 2025 counted 462 reports of small-ads fraud and describes these hurried fake buyers ([BACS: Halbjahresbericht 2025/II](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/O6yLBijwgyl8/BACS-HJB-2025-2_DE.pdf)).

## The safe way to sell

BACS gives clear advice ([BACS: Woche 40](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/25w40-de); [BACS: Sicher online unterwegs](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/sicher-online-unterwegs)):

- **Use your own payment method.** Insist on the way of paying you chose, not the buyer’s link.
- **No card, no code.** Receiving money never needs your card details or a confirmation code.
- **Check your own account.** Look in your banking or TWINT app yourself. Don’t trust screenshots, PDFs or emails that say “paid”.
- **Ship only after the money has arrived.**
- **Never open files from buyers.** Treat them as harmful, especially ones that say you must open them to confirm a payment.
- **Never type your e-banking login or card details on a so-called shipping portal** after a sale.

## If you already clicked

If you opened a file from a buyer, BACS advises: disconnect the device from the internet, change your passwords from another, clean device, and report it to BACS and the police ([BACS: Woche 40](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/25w40-de)). If you entered card details, call your card company at once to block the card.

:::helper
If a relative sells things online, agree on one simple rule with them: “If a buyer sends a link, a QR code or a file, stop and call me.” Sit with them for their first sale and check the payment arrive in their own app together.
:::end
