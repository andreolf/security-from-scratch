:::story Ten minutes at the kitchen table
After the family lunch, Walter and his grandson Luca sit at the kitchen table with Walter’s phone. “It’s only a few taps,” Luca says. “And you press them, not me.”

Ten minutes later Walter has two-step sign-in on his email. Luca shows him what happens next time he signs in on a new device: the phone asks “Is it you trying to sign in?” Walter taps “Yes”. “And if I didn’t start it?” “Then you tap ‘No’, and you call me.”

*Walter and Luca are made up.*
:::end

:::remember
**Turn on two-step sign-in for your email first. Make sure the phone number on the account is your current one.**
:::end

## Before you start

- Have your phone charged, and know your email password (your password manager can show it, lesson 03.04).
- Check which service your email is with. Gmail addresses use a Google Account. iCloud addresses use your Apple Account. For other providers, look in their settings for “Security” or “Two-step”.
- Sit down somewhere calm. There is no hurry and nothing will break.

:::try Turn on two-step sign-in
Choose the tab for the account your email uses. The steps are checked against the official help pages; menu names can change slightly with new software.

--- iPhone
For an Apple Account (iCloud email):
1. Open **Settings**, then tap **your name** at the top.
2. Tap **Sign-In & Security**.
3. Tap **Two-Factor Authentication** and turn it on. If it already says “On”, you are done: most Apple Accounts already use it.
4. Follow the steps on the screen. You confirm a **trusted phone number**, where codes can be sent.

Checked against: [Apple Support: Two-factor authentication for Apple Account](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102660).

--- Android
For a Google Account (Gmail):
1. Open the **Settings** app, then tap **Google**, then **Manage your Google Account**.
2. At the top, tap **Security & sign-in**.
3. Under “How you sign in to Google”, tap **Turn on 2-Step Verification**.
4. Follow the on-screen steps.

Checked against: [Google Account Help: Turn on 2-Step Verification](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/185839?co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid&hl=en) and [Google Account Help: Sign in with backup codes](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/1187538?co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid&hl=en).
:::end

## What changes afterwards

Not much, day to day. On your own phone you usually stay signed in. When you, or anyone else, signs in on a new device, you will be asked for the second step. Apple shows a six-digit code on your other devices or sends it to your trusted phone number ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102660)).

If you are ever asked for a second step and you did **not** just sign in yourself, someone else has your password. Say “No”, then change your email password.

:::exercise Practise the “No”
Imagine your phone shows: “Are you trying to sign in? Zurich, Windows computer.” You are sitting on your sofa with only your phone. What do you do?
:::solution
Tap “No” or “It wasn’t me”. You did not start that sign-in, so someone else has your password. Then change your email password using your password manager, and tell your helper. Lesson 04.02’s rule also applies: never give the code to anyone who calls about it.
:::end

:::helper
Let your relative press every button. Check the trusted phone number is theirs and current. If they have a second phone or a tablet, it can act as a backup. Set up a spare way back in, as in lesson 04.05, on the same day.
:::end
