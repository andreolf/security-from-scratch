:::story The friendly inspector
Walter is 84 and lives alone in a village near St. Gallen. A calm man calls. “Inspector Keller, cantonal police. We arrested a gang of burglars. They had a list, and your name was on it.”

The inspector says some bank staff are working with the gang. “Your savings are not safe. Please don’t talk to anyone at the bank.” Tomorrow, he says, a plain-clothes colleague will come to collect cash and jewellery for safekeeping.

Walter feels proud to help the police. But one thing bothers him: the police want his money. He hangs up, waits for the dial tone and calls 117. The real police tell him: there is no Inspector Keller, and nobody will come. He did everything right.
:::end

:::remember
**Real police never ask for money or valuables, and never send anyone to collect them. Hang up and call 117 yourself.**
:::end

## How the trick works

This scam is common in Switzerland and is often called “falsche Polizisten” (fake police officers). The St. Gallen cantonal police describe the usual steps: the caller says he is the police, asks you to cooperate, claims bank staff work with criminals, asks you to prepare cash or valuables, and then sends a “courier” ([Kantonspolizei St.Gallen](https://www.sg.ch/sicherheit/kantonspolizei/praevention--jugend--cybercrime/cybercrime/telefonbetrug.html)).

Zurich police add that the callers say your money is “not safe at the bank”, and that the number on your screen can be faked, so it can even look like a real police number ([cybercrimepolice.ch](https://cybercrimepolice.ch/de/faelle/falsche-polizisten-rufen-wieder-aeltere-personen-an)). Lesson 01.05 explains how that works.

## Warning signs

- The “police” call you about your **money, savings or valuables**.
- They say your **bank can’t be trusted**, or ask you not to talk to bank staff.
- They ask you to **withdraw cash**, buy gold, or move money.
- Someone offers to **come to your home** to collect money or jewellery.
- They ask you to **keep it secret**.

## What real police do

Real police never ask you for money by phone and never send people to pick up cash or valuables ([cybercrimepolice.ch](https://cybercrimepolice.ch/de/faelle/schockanruf-durch-falsche-polizisten)). They will never mind if you hang up and call them back. If you are unsure, call 117. On a landline, hang up first, wait for the dial tone, and then dial, so you are not still connected to the fake caller.

## Some good news

Swiss authorities report that threatening calls in the name of authorities, such as fake police, fell clearly in the second half of 2025 ([BACS half-year report](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/hjb-25-2-de)). Every person who hangs up helps make this trick less worthwhile.

:::practice call-drill scenario=police

:::helper
Tell your parent or grandparent: “If the police ever call about your money, it is a scam. Hang up and call 117.” If they live alone, a short sign next to the phone helps: “Police never collect money. Call 117.” You can also visit the bank together and ask what protection it offers against large, unusual cash withdrawals.
:::end
