:::story Practice at the kitchen table
Every Sunday, Anna visits her mother, Ruth, who is 86. Last Sunday they tried something new. Anna pretended to be a pushy caller. “Mrs Weber, this is urgent. You must decide now!”

Ruth laughed the first time and didn’t know what to say. The second time, she said: “I never decide about money on the phone. Goodbye.” By the fourth time, it came out calm and easy. “It’s like learning a song,” Ruth said. “Once you know the words, you don’t have to think.”
:::end

:::remember
**You never have to explain, argue or be polite to a stranger who wants money. “Goodbye” is a complete answer.**
:::end

## Why practise?

Many of us were raised to be polite and helpful. We don’t like hanging up on people. Scammers count on that. They keep talking, they sound hurt, they get angry, or they say you will regret it.

When you have practised a few sentences, you don’t need to find words under pressure. You simply say what you already know.

The St. Gallen police put it in three words: “Legen Sie einfach auf!” (“Just hang up!”) ([Kantonspolizei St.Gallen](https://www.sg.ch/sicherheit/kantonspolizei/praevention--jugend--cybercrime/cybercrime/telefonbetrug.html)). The US Federal Communications Commission says the same: “Don’t Hang On, Hang Up!” ([FCC](https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/spoofing)).

## Sentences that end any call

Choose one or two that feel natural to you.

- “I never decide about money on the phone. Goodbye.”
- “I will call you back on the official number.”
- “I never read out codes to anyone.”
- “I talk about everything with my family first.”
- “I am hanging up now.”

You don’t need to wait for an answer. Say the sentence, then put the phone down. If the caller was real, they will understand. If they were not, you have just protected yourself.

## When the caller gets angry

Scammers often become rude or threatening when you try to leave. That is a sign that you are doing the right thing. Real officials stay calm and are happy for you to call them back.

:::practice say-no

:::exercise A role-play with family
Ask a family member or friend to be the caller. They read one of the pushy lines from the practice cards above, in a pushy voice. You answer with your calm sentence and “hang up”. Swap roles once, so you can hear how it sounds from the other side. Do it three or four times.
:::solution
After a few rounds, most people find that the sentence comes out without thinking. That is the goal. Repeat the role-play now and then, for example once a month, so it stays fresh.
:::end

:::helper
Keep the role-play light and fun. Never make it a test. Praise the calm answer, not a “perfect” one. Agree that your relative can always say “I will call my son/daughter first”, and then actually call you. Your calm reaction teaches them it is always fine to check.
:::end
