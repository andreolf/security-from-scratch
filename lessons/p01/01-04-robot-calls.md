:::story The voice in English
Giorgio is 73 and lives in Lugano. His phone shows a Swiss mobile number. When he answers, a recorded voice speaks in English: “This is the police. Your identity has been used in a crime. Press 1 to speak to an officer.”

Giorgio doesn’t understand everything, and that makes him nervous. His finger is already above the 1. Then he remembers: real police don’t call with a recording. He presses nothing and hangs up. The same number calls twice more that afternoon. He doesn’t answer.
:::end

:::remember
**Real police never call you with a recording. Press nothing. Hang up.**
:::end

## How the trick works

These calls play a recorded message. It claims to be the police, Interpol, Europol or another authority. Zurich police note that the computer voice usually speaks English ([cybercrimepolice.ch](https://cybercrimepolice.ch/de/faelle/vorsicht-bei-telefonanrufen-mit-bandansagen)). Hearing a language you don’t speak well adds to the stress.

Zurich police explain the next steps: if you press 1, you are connected to a fake officer. The calls often appear to come from Swiss mobile numbers, which are faked with internet telephony. The goal is often to get you to install a remote-control program on your computer or phone ([cybercrimepolice.ch: Bandansagen](https://cybercrimepolice.ch/de/faelle/vorsicht-bei-telefonanrufen-mit-bandansagen)).

## What to do

- **Press nothing.** Not 1, not 9, not “to be removed from the list”.
- **Hang up.** The US Federal Communications Commission says: if a recording asks you to press a button, just hang up ([FCC](https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/spoofing)).
- **Don’t call back.** The number may belong to an innocent person whose number was faked.
- **Never allow remote access, and never give voucher or gift card codes** to anyone who called you ([cybercrimepolice.ch](https://cybercrimepolice.ch/de/faelle/vorsicht-bei-telefonanrufen-mit-bandansagen)).

The FCC also suggests not answering yes-or-no questions from unknown callers ([FCC](https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/spoofing)). You don’t owe a recording any answer at all.

:::try Fewer calls from unknown numbers
You can ask your phone to screen or silence calls from numbers that are not in your contacts. Important calls from new numbers then go to voicemail, so check your voicemail now and then.

--- iPhone
1. Open the **Settings** app.
2. Tap **Apps**, then tap **Phone**.
3. In the section for unknown numbers, choose **Ask Reason for Calling** (the caller must say who they are first) or **Silence** (the call goes to voicemail).
4. To block one number: open the **Phone** app, tap **Contacts**, tap the contact, then tap **Block Contact**.

Checked against: [Apple Support: Screen and block calls on iPhone](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/screen-and-block-calls-iphe4b3f7823/ios).

--- Android
1. Open the **Phone** app.
2. Tap **More options** (three dots), then **Settings**, then **Caller ID and spam**.
3. Make sure **See caller ID & spam** is on.
4. To block spam calls, turn on **Filter spam calls**.
5. To report one call: in the call history, tap the call, then tap **Block or Report spam**.

These steps are for the Phone by Google app. Other brands may use different words. Checked against: [Google Phone app Help: Use caller ID & spam protection](https://support.google.com/phoneapp/answer/3459196).
:::end

:::helper
Robot calls are confusing when they are in a language your relative doesn’t speak well. Make the rule very simple: “A recording that says ‘police’ is always fake. Press nothing.” If the calls are frequent, set up call screening together, and show how to listen to voicemail.
:::end
