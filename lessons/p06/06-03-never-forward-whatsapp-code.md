:::story “Sent to you by mistake”
Kurt is 80 and lives in Schaffhausen. A text message arrives with a WhatsApp code he didn’t ask for. A moment later, his bowling friend Peter writes on WhatsApp: “Sorry Kurt, I sent my code to your number by mistake. Could you forward it to me?”

Kurt nearly does it. Then he thinks: why would Peter’s code come to *my* phone? He calls Peter, the old-fashioned way. Peter has no idea what he is talking about. His WhatsApp had been taken over that morning.

*Kurt and Peter are made up.*
:::end

:::remember
**Never send a WhatsApp code to anyone. If it arrives without you asking, someone is trying to take over your account.**
:::end

## Why the code is so valuable

WhatsApp sends you a code by text message when someone tries to set up WhatsApp with your phone number. WhatsApp explains that this can be a typing mistake, but it can also happen when someone attempts to take over your account. Its advice is clear: never share your WhatsApp verification code with others. Without it, nobody can use your number on WhatsApp ([WhatsApp Help Center: Received verification code without requesting it](https://faq.whatsapp.com/479314433984258/?locale=en_US)).

## Why the request comes from a friend

The criminals often write from an account they have already taken over. The Zurich cantonal police explain that you can’t easily tell: the messages appear in your existing chat, with your friend’s usual number and profile picture. The criminals then ask contacts for money, post money requests in the WhatsApp Status, or send links to fake websites to take over more accounts ([cybercrimepolice.ch: Übernommene WhatsApp-Konten](https://cybercrimepolice.ch/de/faelle/ubernommene-whatsapp-konten-betruger-versenden-links-oder-fordern-geld)).

So the rule doesn’t depend on who asks. Nobody needs your code: not a friend, not “WhatsApp support”, not the police.

## If you already sent it

Act quickly ([cybercrimepolice.ch](https://cybercrimepolice.ch/de/faelle/ubernommene-whatsapp-konten-betruger-versenden-links-oder-fordern-geld), [WhatsApp Help Center](https://faq.whatsapp.com/834124628020911/?cms_platform=android&locale=en_US)):

1. In WhatsApp, open **Linked devices** and log out any device you don’t know.
2. If you are locked out, set up WhatsApp again with your own phone number and follow the recovery steps.
3. Tell your contacts another way (a call, a text, an email) that your account was hacked, so they don’t send money.
4. Turn on two-step verification (lesson 06.02).

:::helper
Explain the rule in one line: “A code that arrives on your phone is only for you, always.” Put it on the trusted-numbers card. If your relative’s friend’s account is taken over, help them warn others by phone.
:::end
