:::remember
**A friend asking for money or pushing an investment online may not be your friend. Check another way, by phone.**
:::end

## Two kinds of fake friends

**Copied profiles.** A criminal makes a new account with your friend’s public photo and name, then sends friend requests to their contacts. If you’re already friends with that person, a second request is a warning sign.

**Hacked accounts.** Worse: the criminal gets into your friend’s real account. The US Federal Trade Commission explains that scammers can hack your profile, pretend to be you and con your friends. It advises: don’t reply to messages that ask for money or personal information, even if they seem to come from a friend or family member, because their account may have been hacked. Call them to check ([FTC: That friend request could be from a scammer](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/10/friend-request-could-be-scammer)).

The Swiss Federal Office for Cyber Security notes that attackers often use hacked accounts specifically to target the person’s contacts ([BACS: Social Media Hacking](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/social-media-hacking-de)).

## The “friend” who suddenly invests

A common sign of a hacked account: an old friend suddenly posts about making easy money, often with cryptocurrency or a payment app, and invites you to join. The FTC says this is likely an investment scam run from a taken-over account, and suggests sending your real friend a quick email or text in case they were hacked ([FTC: Can you spot an investment scam on social media?](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2025/05/can-you-spot-investment-scam-social-media)).

## Report the fake

Facebook doesn’t allow profiles that pretend to be someone else. To report one: go to the profile, choose **Options** below the cover photo, then **Report profile**, and follow the steps. You can report even without a Facebook account ([Facebook Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/help/167722253287296)).

If **your own** account was hacked, BACS advises changing your password at once, using the provider’s recovery process if you’re locked out, and warning your contacts through another channel ([BACS](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/social-media-hacking-de)).

:::helper
Agree on a family rule: “We never ask each other for money by message.” If your relative’s account is ever hacked, help them through the provider’s recovery steps, then turn on two-step sign-in (Phase 04).
:::end
