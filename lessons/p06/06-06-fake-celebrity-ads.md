:::story The interview that never happened
Ruth is 72 and lives in Lausanne. On a news site she sees what looks like an article from a big Swiss newspaper: a well-known TV presenter says she got rich with a new trading platform, starting with just 250 francs. There is a video too, with her face and voice.

Ruth is tempted. Then she checks the address bar: it isn’t the newspaper’s website at all. She closes it and tells her son, who finds the same “interview” with three other famous people.

*Ruth is made up. These fake articles are real.*
:::end

:::remember
**Famous people don’t sell investments in adverts. A news page can be copied, and a face or voice can be faked.**
:::end

## How the scam works

The Swiss Federal Office for Cyber Security describes it in detail. Websites styled like well-known media (often Blick, 20 Minuten, SRF and RTS) show made-up interviews with famous people, such as federal councillors, athletes or TV presenters. They promise a fortune in a short time from a small investment, usually 250 francs or euros. The ads appear in spam, on social media such as X and Facebook, and in YouTube adverts ([BACS: Woche 7](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/25w07-de)).

After you register, a trained “personal adviser” calls you quickly. A website shows your money growing, and you are pushed to pay much more. The money is never invested. When you try to withdraw it, you are asked for more “fees” ([BACS](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/25w07-de)). The Zurich cantonal police report the same pattern, with a call from the “broker” often within minutes ([cybercrimepolice.ch: Promibilder](https://cybercrimepolice.ch/de/faelle/online-anlagebetrueger-missbrauchen-prominente)).

## Faces and voices can be faked

Some of these ads use real photos taken out of context. Others use AI to make a video or voice that looks and sounds real. The AI Safety from Scratch site explains how such “deepfakes” work. The safe rule is simple: you don’t need to tell real from fake. A money tip in an advert is a scam, whoever seems to give it.

## How to check, and what to do

- **Check the address.** Make sure you are really on the newspaper’s own website, through your bookmark (lesson 02.04).
- **Check the company with FINMA.** In Switzerland, financial firms need a licence. FINMA keeps a list of authorised firms and a warning list of companies that are not authorised and may be operating without a licence ([FINMA: Warning list](https://www.finma.ch/en/finma-public/warnungen/warnliste)).
- **Too good to be true is not true.** BACS reminds us that high profits from minimal investment and no work are too good to be true ([BACS](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/25w07-de)).
- **Already paid?** Stop all contact, make no further payments (not even “fees” to get money back), be wary of “lawyers” who later offer to recover it for a fee, and report it to the cantonal police ([BACS](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/25w07-de)).

:::practice scenes scene=celeb

:::helper
Talk about money tips openly in the family: “If you ever want to invest, let’s look at it together first.” There is no shame in having clicked. If money has gone, help your relative call the bank and the police, and watch out for “recovery” offers afterwards.
:::end
