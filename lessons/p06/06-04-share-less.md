:::story The birthday post
Gisela is 80 and lives in Olten. Her grandson posts on Facebook: “Happy 80th birthday, Oma Gisela! Enjoy Mallorca with Opa and Bello the dog!” It is public, with a photo of the house.

Her daughter sees it and gently asks him to make it “friends only”. “Now everyone knows her age, that the house is empty for two weeks, and the dog’s name. And Oma uses Bello in her passwords.” The grandson changes it at once, and Gisela changes her password that evening.

*Gisela is made up.*
:::end

:::remember
**Before you post, ask: could a stranger use this? Keep posts for friends only, and share holidays after you’re back.**
:::end

## Scammers do their homework

The Swiss Federal Office for Cyber Security warns that scammers often collect personal information on social networks to make their attacks more personal. It advises sharing personal details with restraint, and reminds us that the internet forgets nothing ([BACS: Réseaux sociaux](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/fr/reseaux-sociaux-ne-divulguez-pas-trop-dinformations-personnelles)).

The US Federal Trade Commission adds that scammers use information from your profile, like your age and hobbies, to target you ([FTC: That friend request could be from a scammer](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/10/friend-request-could-be-scammer)).

## Three things to keep to yourself

- **When you are away.** The Valais cantonal police advise: don’t share your absence on social networks ([Kantonspolizei Wallis](https://www.polizeiwallis.ch/medienmitteilungen/lassen-sie-einbrecher-nicht-von-ihren-ferien-profitieren/)). Post the holiday photos when you’re back.
- **Birthdays, pets’ names, your home town.** These are easy to find and easy to guess. CISA warns that birthdays and pet names are not safe as passwords ([CISA: Use strong passwords](https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world/use-strong-passwords)).
- **Lots of photos and voice clips in public.** BACS notes that the more pictures are available, the easier it is to make fake images, so think about what each post reveals ([BACS: Woche 32](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/24w32-de)).

## Friends only

BACS recommends setting the platform so that only people on your friends list can see your photos ([BACS](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/24w32-de)). The FTC also advises using your privacy settings to limit who can see your information and posts ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/10/friend-request-could-be-scammer)). Each network names these settings a little differently; look for “Privacy” or “Audience” in its settings, and ask your helper if you can’t find them.

:::exercise Look at your own profile like a stranger
Open your social media profile and imagine you are a scammer. Write down three things you could learn about yourself: where you live, family names, your pet, your birthday, when you are away.
:::solution
Most people find more than they expected. For each item, decide: delete it, change it to “friends only”, or keep it. And if a pet’s name or a birthday is part of one of your passwords, change that password using your password manager (lesson 03.03).
:::end

:::helper
Ask before posting about your relative, and especially before posting their age, address or travel plans. Help them check the audience setting of their own posts. Agree as a family: holiday photos go online after everyone is home.
:::end

:::quiz
- q: You are going on holiday for two weeks. When should you post photos publicly?
  options: ["Every day while away", "After you are back home, and preferably for friends only", "Before you leave, with the dates"]
  answer: 1
  explain: Don’t announce that your home is empty.
- q: Why shouldn’t you use your pet’s name in a password?
  options: ["It may be on social media and is easy to guess", "Pets don’t like it", "Passwords can’t contain names"]
  answer: 0
  explain: Personal details like pet names and birthdays are easy to find.
- q: What is the safest audience for personal posts?
  options: ["Public", "Friends only", "Everyone and their friends"]
  answer: 1
  explain: Fewer strangers see it, so fewer scammers can use it.
:::end
