:::story A broken phone
Elisabeth is 78 and lives in Bern. A WhatsApp message from an unknown number: “Hi Mum, my phone is broken, this is my new number. Can you save it?” It must be Daniel, she thinks. He’s always losing things.

The next day: “Mum, I have to pay a bill today and my banking doesn’t work on the new phone. Could you transfer CHF 1,850? I’ll pay you back on Friday.” Elisabeth feels uneasy. She calls Daniel’s old number. He answers at once, from his perfectly working phone. “Mum, that’s not me!”

*Elisabeth and Daniel are made up. Messages like this are real.*
:::end

:::remember
**A “new number” asking for money is a scam until you’ve checked. Call your family on the number you already have.**
:::end

## How the trick works

The Zurich cantonal police describe it like this: a message claims to come from your son or daughter, whose phone is broken, so they can only be reached on WhatsApp at a new number. Words like “It’s important” build pressure. After a few friendly messages, they urgently need money for a bill or a transfer, and give you payment instructions: a bank transfer, a payment service, or voucher and prepaid codes ([cybercrimepolice.ch: «Hallo Mama»](https://cybercrimepolice.ch/de/faelle/hallo-mama-mail-lockt-auf-whatsapp)).

The same police page shows a new twist: the first message can even arrive as an email that looks like a WhatsApp notification, with a button to “open” the chat ([cybercrimepolice.ch](https://cybercrimepolice.ch/de/faelle/hallo-mama-mail-lockt-auf-whatsapp)).

## The safe check

- **Don’t save the new number,** and don’t delete the old one.
- **Call your child on the old number.** A real conversation usually clears it up in seconds.
- **Ask a control question** only your child could answer. The police add: choose a question whose answer isn’t online or on social media ([cybercrimepolice.ch](https://cybercrimepolice.ch/de/faelle/hallo-mama-mail-lockt-auf-whatsapp)).
- **Never send money because of a message.** If family needs help, settle it in a real call.

The US Federal Trade Commission gives the same advice for fake family emergencies: hang up and call back on a number you know ([FTC: Fake emergencies](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/scammers-use-fake-emergencies-steal-your-money)).
