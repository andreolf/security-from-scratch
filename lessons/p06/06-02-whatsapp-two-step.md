:::story Rosa’s lucky lock
Rosa is 73 and lives in Chur. Last spring a friend’s WhatsApp was taken over, and the criminals wrote to everyone in her contacts asking for money. Rosa’s granddaughter set up WhatsApp’s two-step verification on Rosa’s phone that weekend.

“Now, if anyone ever gets your code,” she explained, “they still need a password that only you know.” Rosa wrote the password on the paper with her backup codes, in the folder with her passport.

*Rosa is made up.*
:::end

:::remember
**Turn on two-step verification in WhatsApp. It adds a password only you know, so a code alone can’t take over your account.**
:::end

## What it does

To set up WhatsApp on a new phone, you need a code that WhatsApp sends to your number by text message. Criminals try to trick people into giving them that code (lesson 06.03). Two-step verification adds a second lock: WhatsApp calls it an optional feature that adds more security, and protects against unauthorised access by using two different forms of authentication ([WhatsApp Help Center: About two-step verification](https://faq.whatsapp.com/1278661612895630)).

## A password, not a PIN

You may remember a 6-digit PIN. WhatsApp writes that it is upgrading the 6-digit PIN used in two-step verification to a password ([WhatsApp Help Center: Manage two-step verification](https://faq.whatsapp.com/1920866721452534/?cms_platform=android&locale=en_US)). Your password manager (lesson 03.03) can create and keep it, or write it on paper at home with your backup codes.

:::try Turn on two-step verification in WhatsApp
These steps are inside the WhatsApp app. They are checked against the WhatsApp Help Center; the app changes often, so names may differ slightly.

--- WhatsApp on iPhone
1. Open WhatsApp and tap **You** (or **Settings**), then **Account**, then **Two-step verification**.
2. Turn on **Turn on two-step verification**.
3. Create a strong password and confirm it.
4. Add an email address you can access (this helps if you forget the password), and tap **Next**. Or tap **Skip**.
5. Tap **Trust this device**, or **Skip**.

Checked against: [WhatsApp Help Center: How to manage two-step verification settings (iPhone)](https://faq.whatsapp.com/1920866721452534/?cms_platform=iphone&locale=en_US).

--- WhatsApp on Android
1. Open WhatsApp and tap the **three dots** at the top, then **Settings**, **Account**, **Two-step verification**.
2. Turn on **Turn on two-step verification**.
3. Create a strong password and confirm it.
4. Add an email address you can access and tap **Next**, or tap **Skip**.
5. Tap **Trust this device**, or **Skip**.

Checked against: [WhatsApp Help Center: How to manage two-step verification settings (Android)](https://faq.whatsapp.com/1920866721452534/?cms_platform=android&locale=en_US).
:::end

## While you are there: check linked devices

WhatsApp can also run on a computer or tablet that is “linked” to your phone. The Zurich cantonal police describe a scam where a fake “vote” website asks you to scan a QR code with WhatsApp, which secretly links your account to the criminals’ device ([cybercrimepolice.ch: Gefälschte Tanzabstimmung](https://cybercrimepolice.ch/de/faelle/gefaelschte-tanzabstimmung-fuehrt-zu-zugriff-auf-das-whatsapp-konto)).

Look at **Linked devices** in WhatsApp (in the three-dots menu on Android, or under Settings on iPhone). If you see a device you don’t recognise, tap it and tap **Log out** ([WhatsApp Help Center: How to unlink a device](https://faq.whatsapp.com/834124628020911/?cms_platform=android&locale=en_US)).

:::exercise Who knows your WhatsApp password?
After turning it on, answer honestly: where is the password written down, and who else knows it?
:::solution
Best: it is in your password manager, or on paper at home with your backup codes, and nobody else knows it. Type it only in the WhatsApp app itself, and never give it to anyone who asks for it, whoever they say they are.
:::end

:::helper
Do it together on their phone, and let them choose and type the password. Add an email address they actually use. Check Linked devices together, and log out of anything they don’t recognise, such as an old computer.
:::end
